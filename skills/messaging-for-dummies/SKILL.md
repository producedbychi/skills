# Messaging for Dummies

Help a buyer understand the offer, recognize whether it fits their situation, assess the evidence, and choose a next step. Reduce the effort required to understand the message while preserving its accuracy and the brand's voice. The name is playful. Write to readers as capable people with different knowledge, reading abilities, and available attention.

This skill produces messaging. It can supply a brief for visual production, but a messaging brief is not a rendered ad or a complete marketing plan.

## 1. Establish the assignment

Read the request, relevant conversation, supplied copy, and approved brand context. If a product-marketing context document is available in the project, use it. The skill works without other installed skills.

Treat earlier AI drafts as proposals. Carry forward a factual claim only when its source supports it or the user has confirmed it as a fact. Approval of a headline or creative direction establishes that choice, not every process, capability, or result mentioned in the draft. Keep unconfirmed claims provisional.

For supplied copy, revise that artifact directly. For new messaging, establish the offer and buyer situation before drafting. Treat a request for a research-backed audience profile as a research assignment. Read [buyer research](references/buyer-research.md) for that branch; ordinary copy edits do not require a research pass.

Identify the intended audience, offer, placement, desired action, and facts that constrain the wording. Ask one focused question when a missing answer would change the offer, target reader, or material promise. Use the host's native question interface when available. Reuse answers already in context. Proceed with labeled assumptions when the user requests a quick or provisional draft.

Keep the assignment within the requested scope. Editing a headline does not require auditing the whole business or developing a campaign.

Complete this step when the artifact and its intended reader are clear, and any consequential unknown is answered or explicitly provisional.

## 2. Identify buyer relevance

Use available evidence to connect the offer to a recognizable situation. Keep these distinctions in the working notes:

- **Belief:** What does the buyer say matters to them? Prefer a quote or a supplied priority.
- **Behavior:** What have they tried, bought, delayed, or asked about? Describe the observation and its context.
- **Language:** What words do they use for the problem, desired outcome, and hesitation?
- **Decision:** What are they comparing, including their current approach or doing nothing? What information or reassurance would help them choose?

Label insights as observed, supplied, or hypothetical. Observed means a direct statement or behavior in an inspected research source or supplied raw excerpt; identify that source. Supplied means a brand fact or audience assertion reported by the user or brand. Hypothetical means an interpretation that still needs buyer evidence. A supplied belief about customers is not an observed customer belief. Separate the brand's facts from its assumptions about customers.

An individual quote establishes that person's view. Broader audience claims need repeated evidence from relevant people. Public or competitor evidence is a proxy unless it concerns this brand's own buyers. Use research quotes to inform the message; publish attributed testimonials only when approved for that use. Use demographic details when they explain the decision; otherwise prioritize the buyer's situation and needs.

Use identity language when buyers themselves express a relevant aspiration or shared value. Otherwise describe the task or situation. Explain suitability through needs and actual constraints, with respect for people who are not a fit. Give buyers information for a voluntary decision. Support urgency with a real deadline or availability limit, and reassurance with a real process, term, or proof asset.

For a narrow rewrite with no customer evidence, preserve the approved audience and promise. Improve comprehension rather than inventing a buyer psychology profile.

Complete this step when the message has a relevant buyer situation and each audience inference has an evidence label.

## 3. Resolve the offer and its ambiguities

Determine which facts the reader needs at this placement:

- What product, service, or action is being offered?
- Who is it for, and in what situation is it useful?
- What does the buyer receive, and which outcome is intended rather than guaranteed?
- What evidence supports the important claims?
- What limits, eligibility rules, prices, or conditions affect the decision?
- What happens after the reader takes the requested action?

Use these as questions, not a required layout. An ad may introduce the offer and lead to details elsewhere. A landing page or proposal may need substantial explanation. Essential qualifications must remain close enough to the claim that the message cannot reasonably imply a broader promise.

For a service ad built around a customer's product or project, identify the service in the requested copy unless supplied adjacent copy already identifies it. Describing the customer's product alone leaves the service being sold unclear.

Find terms that invite materially different interpretations, such as "full service," "unlimited," "fast," "results," or "starting at." Resolve them with supplied specifics. When those specifics are missing, narrow the claim or ask for the fact. Put unresolved details in notes, not fabricated assurances in publishable copy. Treat measurable outcomes, guarantees, testimonials, comparisons, prices, and deadlines as factual claims requiring support.

Preserve permission, certainty, and coverage. Compare every added phrase that implies quantity, scope, or a result with the supplied facts, including playful phrases. Keep conditional access conditional, such as "may receive" rather than "we will provide." Use "all" or "every" only when that coverage is supported.

Complete this step when the draft can state the actual offer without implying unsupported deliverables, outcomes, or conditions.

## 4. Write for understanding

When drafting or revising copy, read [natural copy](references/natural-copy.md). Apply its editing rules during this writing step and the existing interpretation check. This reference is self-contained; it requires no separately installed Unslop skill or additional review workflow.

Choose the main point this artifact should communicate and the action it should support. Lead with the information that helps this buyer recognize the offer. Explain unfamiliar terms where they are needed. Preserve technical terms that carry useful precision for this audience. Support complex concepts with a short example or visual suggestion when words alone leave the relationship unclear.

Write in the supplied brand voice. Plain language can be warm, premium, playful, or technical. Clarity does not require a generic tone, a stock headline formula, an eyebrow, or a fixed number of sections.

Use reading-grade scores only as an optional diagnostic for sentence and word complexity. They do not establish comprehension, intelligence, factual accuracy, or conversion performance. If the user requests a grade target, aim for it while retaining necessary meaning and qualifications; disclose any tradeoff. The default goal is reader understanding, not a universal sixth-grade score.

For several campaign assets, keep the offer and material claims consistent while adapting the detail to each placement. Use a supplied destination to check that the next step supports the ad's promise. When the destination is unavailable, state the required follow-through as a recommendation, not a completed check. Researching or building additional channels is a separate assignment.

Deliver the requested copy first. Supply alternatives only when requested or when a distinct emphasis would help the user choose. If visual production is requested, hand off the audience, recognizable situation, supportable message, exact approved wording, real proof assets, and desired action. Distinguish this handoff from a generated image.

Complete this step when the copy suits the placement, preserves the offer's conditions, and communicates a specific buyer-relevant point.

## 5. Check interpretation before delivery

Review the draft once as an unfamiliar reader. Internally restate the offer and next step using only the artifact and reader-visible context, such as adjacent copy. Treat internal briefs as source facts, not information the reader has already seen. Compare the interpretation with those facts.

Diagnose the problem before changing the message. Separate misunderstanding from weak relevance, an unwanted outcome, an unsupported difference, or uncertainty about the next step. Repair the identified problem using available evidence. Simpler wording can resolve confusion; it cannot establish demand or create an advantage the offer lacks. For comparative or choice-focused copy, explain the supported reason to choose this offer over the actual alternative.

Check whether:

- The product or service is identifiable at the intended placement.
- The reason it matters is specific enough for the intended buyer.
- The wording preserves facts, claim boundaries, and necessary qualifications.
- Abstract terms, unclear pronouns, or implied relationships allow a consequential alternative reading.
- The requested action and what follows it are understandable.
- The message preserves the brand's voice and the user's approved choices.
- The writing meets the natural-copy guidance without losing facts or useful precision.

Repair any specific failure before presenting the copy. If a required fact remains missing, label the result provisional and identify that fact. Describe this as an editorial check, not a test with actual buyers. For an image-only headline or an early campaign introduction, judge what that asset needs to communicate, not whether it contains a complete sales page. When a supplied visual obscures or contradicts the offer, flag the visual change needed rather than claiming the copy has resolved it.

When actual comprehension validation is requested, read [buyer research](references/buyer-research.md). Claim conversion improvement only from appropriate performance evidence for the tested message, audience, and context.

Follow the requested output format. For a copy-only request with enough facts, return the copy alone. Otherwise deliver the requested artifact and brief notes on consequential changes, assumptions, or missing proof. For new positioning, also explain the buyer insight behind it and its evidence status. Keep working tables and the full checklist internal unless the user requests them.

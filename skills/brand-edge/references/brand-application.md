# Brand application

Read this when the assignment connects positioning to offer design, identity, customer access, or future extensions. Apply only the relevant areas. These are decision prompts, not a mandatory brand checklist or visual template.

## Make the offer support the promise

Identify which change would materially improve the chosen customer's experience. Separate current delivery from proposed changes. Check effort, capacity, costs, dependencies, and relevant professional review before presenting a change as feasible. Treat unknown economics as unknown rather than projecting profitability.

| Business context | Questions that can change the recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Physical product | Does formulation, function, assortment, packaging, or availability address the need? Which claims need product testing or specialist review? |
| Service or studio | Do scope, access to the practitioner, process, approvals, and deliverables support the intended result? Can the actual team and capacity deliver it? |
| Software or digital product | Do the workflow, onboarding, integrations, and support help the relevant user succeed? What does the payer or approver need to accept it? |
| Local business | Do location, hours, booking, accessibility, and the in-person experience support the promise? Does the route to purchase fit the service area? |
| Creator, education, membership, or event | What experience or participation does the audience receive? What is the commitment, and what proof supports the promise? |
| Nonprofit, community, or multi-sided business | Who receives the benefit, who pays or contributes, and who decides? Where do their needs conflict? |

Other models can use the same questions about recipient, offer, delivery, and decision. These categories are examples, not exhaustive divisions.

## Make brand expression recognizable and useful

Describe the desired perception and how it supports the chosen strategy. Preserve approved names, logos, colors, and voice. Propose identity changes only when they solve an identified problem and fall within the assignment.

When naming is requested, consider comprehension, recall, audience fit, and room for the intended business scope. Mark candidates as unvetted until appropriate availability checks occur. A name does not need to describe the product literally.

For visual direction, propose subjects or situations that help the buyer recognize the offer and its use. Products may be demonstrated through their use or result. Services may use real work samples, process, or an outcome the service can support. Keep the service being sold identifiable when depicting a client's product. Abstract imagery needs a clear communication purpose.

Use distinctive elements that can repeat across touchpoints without forcing every asset into one layout. Packaging and interface design can solve practical problems as well as aid recognition. Neither a literal visual pun nor a new logo is required for a sound strategy.

For voice, explain a few behavioral choices with a short example, such as direct explanations with precise technical detail for a specialist buyer. Personality adjectives alone give little guidance. Treat example copy and visual directions as prototypes unless the user approves them.

## Reach customers in a relevant context

Recommend channels or prospective partners because of a demonstrated audience overlap, buying moment, or complementary use. Explain why the context fits and what the business must provide there. Verify named partners' current suitability when researching them. Interest, availability, and a signed agreement are different states.

Separate creating awareness from helping someone evaluate or buy. A referral partner may supply trust while a demonstration supplies product understanding. Existing customer behavior is more useful than selecting a platform because it is popular. Where behavior is unknown, propose a channel hypothesis and a small check rather than a full media plan.

## Extend only when the need carries forward

When expansion is requested, connect a new offer to the same audience's related need and the brand's ability to serve it. Check whether it creates a different buyer, business model, capacity demand, or promise. An appealing audience theme alone does not justify expansion.

## Deliver decisions, not a tactic inventory

Show how the consequential applications make the chosen difference real or recognizable. If the positioning depends on a distinctive experience, prioritize the operational commitments that provide it. Let naming, packaging, visual direction, and customer access express that same choice where relevant. A coordinated set of choices can strengthen the position without matching every graphic or phrase literally.

For consequential applications, use a compact mapping when it helps:

| Strategic reason | Keep or change | Dependency or uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The customer's need or decision criterion. | A specific offer, message, experience, or channel decision. | What must exist or be confirmed before it can work. |

Prioritize the few applications that matter now. Leave the rest outside the assignment.

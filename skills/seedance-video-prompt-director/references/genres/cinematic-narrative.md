# Cinematic narrative

> **Lens note.** The camera stacks in this file are grade, stock and finish templates. Their millimeter values are legacy — convert the lens to a FOV degree from the ladder in `optics.md` before writing (`47° (50mm)`, `29° (85mm)`, `84° (24mm)`). Millimeters never lead, and apertures, ISO and lens brand names control nothing.


Character and story are the hero. Controlled craft drives the camera. Use as base genre for: short films, narrative ad creative, branded content with a story spine, dramatic explainers, character-driven testimonials, music video narrative segments. Use as modifier for: any other genre where craftful camera and character depth elevate the base register.

---

## Structural priorities (when used as base)

1. **Character is the resolution beat.** The scene resolves on a face, a gesture, a transformation in the protagonist — not on a product or an event.
2. **Camera is craft.** Every camera move is deliberate and serves the read. No "stabilized slow push-in" defaults — match the camera energy to the emotional register of each beat.
3. **Subtext over text.** Show the change in physical behavior, not in dialogue. The beat where the character realizes something should be a face beat, not a line read.
4. **Restraint at the climax.** The peak moment often holds rather than escalates. A held look beats a shouted line.

## Verb register (Tier 2 — Active / present, with selective Tier 3 punctuation)

Default: walk, step, turn, reach, glance, watch, study, settle, rest, breathe, exhale, lean, lift, lower, push, pull, place, set, hold.

Punctuation verbs at peak beats: snap (head turn at recognition), drops (shoulders at relief), grips (hand on chair-arm at resolve), tightens (jaw at tension).

The cinematic register lives in the gap between verbs. A held beat where someone "doesn't move, breath holds" reads more cinematic than three sentences of action.

---

## Camera grammar defaults

- **Establishing:** wide locked-off or slow crane descent — never handheld unless intentional unease
- **Character beats:** medium close-up at 50mm or 85mm, controlled handheld with micro-jitters or fully locked-off
- **Conversation beats:** OTS at 50mm, alternating between speakers
- **Reaction beats:** push-in slow, sometimes 4+ seconds for a single push
- **Resolution beats:** locked-off, camera holds completely still

Avoid: snap zooms, dutch angles (unless the scene has explicit destabilization), whip pans (unless transitioning between parallel locations).

---

## Lighting defaults

- **Contre-jour backlight as the default separation light** — position the camera on the shadow side of the subject, with atmospheric haze catching the backlight to carve depth
- Practical sources visible in frame (lamps, windows, screens, candles)
- Strong directional key with deep fill ratio (1:8 or steeper for drama)
- Backlight / rim separates subject from background (the contre-jour does this work)
- Catchlights in the eyes consistent across all character coverage
- Available-feeling light even when staged (motivated shadows)

Avoid: flat overhead diffusion (reads commercial), heavy fog (unless mood-specific), volumetric god-rays (unless the scene calls for transcendence).

---

## Color grade

- **60:30:10 color discipline** — one dominant tone carries ~60% of the frame, a secondary supporting tone ~30%, a single accent ~10%. Build the palette deliberately before grading.
- Kodak Portra warmth on skin
- Deep preserved shadows (not crushed, but rich)
- Slight magenta in highlights, slight teal in shadows (classic cinematic)
- Halation on highlights
- Fine grain (35mm film aesthetic)
- Controlled saturation — no clipping, no neon excess

Avoid: high-saturation editorial, clean digital flatness, commercial-bright key + fill.

---

## Skin and texture realism

The face is the hero asset — render it at photographic, not rendered, fidelity.

- **Pore-level skin realism** — visible pores, vellus (peach-fuzz) hair, asymmetric moles and freckles, capillary flush at cheeks, ears, and nose
- **Pore-shadow direction matches the on-set key** — skin texture reads three-dimensional under the contre-jour backlight, never flattened or waxy
- Wet, living eyes with consistent catchlights; visible breath and chest rise
- Motivated micro-sweat sheen or skin oil where the scene's exertion or heat justifies it
- No plastic/CGI skin, no airbrushed smoothing, no uniform complexion

---

## Beat density and pacing

Cinematic narrative breathes. A 15s scene typically has 3–5 cuts (compared to 6–8 in action or commercial-with-action). Beats are longer, holds are deliberate.

The held beat is the cinematic signature. Where a commercial would cut to the next product, cinematic narrative holds on the face for one more second.

---

## Expression vocabulary

Cinematic narrative leans heavily on transformation expressions (see `references/antislop.md` for the full bank). The face is the most important asset.

For beats of relief: brow softens, corners of the mouth lift, breath releases through the nose, shoulders drop in stages.

For beats of recognition: eyes widen, head pulls back, breath catches, hand moves to the chest or mouth, posture freezes mid-motion.

For beats of resolve: jaw sets, eyes narrow, brow drops, weight shifts forward, breath slows.

Specify the muscle-level signature, not the emotion label. "Brow furrows tight" beats "looks worried."

## Composition with other genres

### As modifier on `commercial-product`
- Adds character depth, controlled camera, brief narrative spine
- Product still wins; cinematic register makes the journey to the product feel earned
- Hayden Valley Scene 1 + Scene 6 buyer arc is exactly this composition

### As modifier on `action-intense`
- Cools the action register; trades verb density for held beats
- Adds character motivation visible in face/gesture beats
- Creates "thinking person's action" feel (Heat, Sicario, Mission Impossible: Fallout)

### As modifier on `horror-thriller`
- Adds craftful camera control to the dread (rather than handheld panic)
- Allows for atmospheric holds where the threat is implied
- The Nightingale, There Will Be Blood, No Country for Old Men direction

### As modifier on `documentary-warm`
- Adds dramatic shaping to observational footage
- The composition pushes documentary toward "narrative documentary" / docu-drama
- Slight risk of breaking documentary's authenticity register — use with care

---

## What this genre does NOT do

- Snap zooms, whip pans, dutch angles (without specific motivation)
- High-energy verb density throughout (uses kinetic verbs sparingly, as punctuation)
- Loud color grades (no neon excess, no commercial-bright)
- Action-driven climaxes (climax is character, not event)
- Fast cut rates (3–5 cuts in 15s, not 6–8)
- 3D-render / game-engine looks, plastic CGI skin, airbrushed smoothing

## Common scenes

- Character at a transition point — moment of recognition, decision, resolve
- Two-person conversation with subtext (not exposition)
- Single-character moment of solitude or reflection
- A face responding to information arriving (a phone, a door, a letter)
- Environmental establishing that sets emotional weather
- Dialogue-driven scenes where the camera does the heavy lifting

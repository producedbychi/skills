# Science / education

> **Lens note.** The camera stacks in this file are grade, stock and finish templates. Their millimeter values are legacy — convert the lens to a FOV degree from the ladder in `optics.md` before writing (`47° (50mm)`, `29° (85mm)`, `84° (24mm)`). Millimeters never lead, and apertures, ISO and lens brand names control nothing.


Knowledge is the hero. CGI clarity over cinematic gloss. Use as base genre for: medical visualizations, anatomy education, scientific process visualization, technical demonstrations, transparent / cutaway views, lab and research content.

---

## Structural priorities (when used as base)

1. **Clarity is non-negotiable.** Every visual exists to teach. If a beat doesn't make the concept clearer, cut it.
2. **CGI realism over cinematic style.** 4K medical CGI, transparent anatomy, scientific accuracy. Not stylized, not artistic.
3. **Sequential logic with transitions.** Each beat leads to the next via natural progression — zoom into smaller scale, follow the process, reveal the inner mechanism.
4. **Educational framing.** The viewer is learning. Composition, scale references, and labels (held as clean negative space for graphic overlay in post) serve comprehension.

---

## Style declaration line patterns

- "4K medical CGI, photoreal anatomical visualization, transparent / cutaway human anatomy, soft directional clinical lighting, scientific accuracy, smooth controlled camera, 16:9."
- "Educational scientific visualization, premium 3D CGI realism, particle simulation accurate to physics, clean studio environment, controlled push-in and orbit camera, neutral color grade, 16:9."
- "Lab / research observational documentary cinematography, ARRI ALEXA Mini for real environments, integrated 4K CGI overlays for invisible processes, clean directional light, 16:9."

---

## Verb register (Tier 2 — controlled / observational)

flow, travel, pass, move, transport, deliver, attach, bind, separate, release, divide, multiply, transform, react, combine, dissolve, crystallize, oscillate, rotate, orbit, propagate, transmit, receive, absorb, emit, catalyze, replicate.

Science verbs are precise. They describe what's happening at the level the viewer can observe.

---

## Camera grammar defaults

- **Establishing:** locked-off wide showing the full subject (organ, organism, system)
- **Detail beats:** stabilized push-in to a specific feature, often through transparent / cutaway view
- **Process beats:** controlled orbit around the subject as a process unfolds
- **Internal beats:** the camera enters the subject (transparent overlay, cutaway reveal)
- **Resolution beat:** pull-back to context, subject in its full scale

Science camera is patient and educational. No handheld, no dutch angles, no whip pans.

---

## Lighting defaults

- Soft clinical / studio diffusion (no dramatic shadows)
- Controlled directional key for slight modeling
- Cool fill from opposite side (medical / lab register)
- True whites preserved (background is often pure white or pale neutral)
- Specular highlights on surfaces only where it serves anatomy / structure visibility

---

## Color grade

- Neutral — slight warm or slight cool, not committed
- True whites for medical / lab register
- Saturation only where it teaches (color-coded systems, contrast media, biological pigment)
- Halation minimal (clinical clarity prioritized)

---

## Atmosphere

Science scenes are atmosphere-clean by default:
- No particulate (dust, smoke, fog kill clarity)
- Air is invisible
- Only purposeful particles (medical contrast, blood cells, gas molecules) when teaching the concept

---

## Visual labeling and graphic overlay

Science prompts often need labels (organ names, chemical reactions, force vectors). Hold these as clean negative space for motion graphics composite in post:

> Composition leaves clean band along the lower third of frame for label / annotation overlay in post-production. Subject is centered in upper two-thirds.

> Negative space surrounding the molecule allows for chemical formula composite at frame-right.

---

## CGI integration with real environments

For lab / research scenes, often the environment is real (the lab, the equipment, the researcher's hands) and the inside view (cell-level, molecule-level) is CGI:

> Open on a real lab bench, microscope at center frame, researcher's hands operating the controls. Push-in transitions through the eyepiece into a 4K CGI representation of the cell sample below — micro-tubule structures resolving as the camera continues its push.

The transition from real to CGI is the science-genre signature beat. Make it visually clear with motion (push, dissolve through occlusion, transparent overlay forming over real footage).

---

## Sound design

Science sound is functional and clean:

```
SFX: ambient lab tone (HVAC, soft equipment hum), single instrument click, soft particle interaction sounds during CGI beats, smooth tonal sweep on scale transitions, return to ambient on resolution.
```

VO is calm, clear, measured. Not theatrical, not dramatized.

## What this genre does NOT do

- Cinematic dramatic camera moves
- Action verb density
- Heavy color grades
- Atmospheric particulate (kills clarity)
- Theatrical performance
- Fast-cut rhythms

---

## Negative prompt block

```
Negative: no watermarks, no subtitles unless explicitly part of the educational design, no atmospheric haze that obscures the subject, no dramatic lighting that creates inaccessible shadows, no fast cuts, no commercial-bright color grade.
```

## Common scenes

- Cell division / mitosis with transparent organelle visibility
- Blood vessel travel (camera inside the artery, blood cells passing)
- Drug action mechanism (molecule binding to receptor)
- Mechanical system cutaway (engine, machine, organ shown internally)
- Particle / chemistry reaction (molecules combining, breaking apart)
- Anatomical layer reveal (skin → muscle → bone, sequential transparency)
- Astronomical scale (zoom out from human to planet to galaxy)
- Geological process (rock formation, weather, plate tectonics)

---

## Scientific accuracy guardrails

If the brief involves real scientific subjects, prioritize accuracy:
- Don't invent fictitious organs, fictional molecules, made-up scales
- Use correct chemistry / biology / physics terminology in the prompt
- If the brief describes a process incorrectly, flag the inaccuracy and ask before generating
- For pharmaceutical / medical content, the brief should be vetted by domain experts before generation

The educational register loses credibility with errors. Take care with the science.

---

## Scale transition patterns

Science genre often spans multiple orders of magnitude. Make the scale transitions visible:

> 0–4s: Wide locked-off on a runner crossing a finish line at full sprint. Camera holds at human-scale.
>
> 4–8s: Push-in toward the runner's chest. Camera continues pushing past the surface skin level into a 4K CGI cutaway of the heart, beating in cross-section.
>
> 8–12s: Continued push into the heart's chambers, individual blood cells visible flowing through. Camera tracks one cell as it passes through the aortic valve.
>
> 12–15s: Pull back through the same path — cell, valve, chamber, surface skin, runner crossing the line, the wide locked-off composition restored.

The closing pull-back is what cements the educational read — the viewer realizes the entire CGI sequence happened "inside" the runner.

# Creative brief mode

The brief defines the decision a treatment must answer. It can start from a client email, a project folder, a conversation, or an existing brief. It does not assume an approved concept, script, or previous campaign exists.

## Gather and resolve

Read supplied goals, audience or customer material, product facts, brand guidance, distribution plans, project assets, previous work, and client feedback. Keep a consequential fact tied to its source. If sources disagree, show the conflict and ask which source governs. Do not convert a mood-board image into a requirement or a proposed target into an approved goal.

Ask only for missing answers that would materially change the work. Common decision points are what the piece must accomplish, who must respond, where it will appear, what must be delivered, budget or timing bounds, and who approves. If the user wants to move quickly, make a clearly labeled working brief from what is known and leave the rest open.

## Brief output

Use a compact format that the next person can act on. Include relevant fields, not empty boilerplate:

| Field | What it must answer |
| --- | --- |
| Project and status | Who is commissioning what, and is this brief working or approved? |
| Objective | What should change for the audience or business? Include a success measure only if supplied or explicitly proposed. |
| Audience and context | Who is the primary viewer, what do they know now, and where will they encounter the work? |
| Core message and proof | What single point should they retain, and which approved facts or demonstrations support it? |
| Desired response | What should the viewer think, feel, or do after watching? |
| Deliverables | Each version's purpose, channel, duration, aspect ratio, language, and call to action when known. |
| Requirements and boundaries | Brand or product requirements, claims, accessibility needs, prohibitions supplied by the client, and technical constraints. |
| Resources and limits | Available people and assets, budget range, deadlines, production limits, and dependencies. |
| Approvals and open decisions | Decision-maker, approval stages, facts still needed, and who will resolve them. |

Distinguish `confirmed`, `proposed`, and `unknown` within the document where the distinction matters. If a project already has a sound brief, summarize its controlling facts rather than rewriting it. If the user requests only a treatment, a short brief snapshot can precede the treatment without becoming a separate deliverable.

## Quality check

- Can a creative lead tell what the work must accomplish, for whom, and where it will run?
- Are the message and proof supportable, with unsupported claims visible?
- Are deliverables specific enough to price and plan, or explicitly unresolved?
- Can the client identify which decisions they must make before approving a treatment?

If the brief contains incompatible goals or impossible scope, name the tradeoff. Do not hide it in a polished concept.

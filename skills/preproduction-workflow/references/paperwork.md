# Paperwork mode

Prepare the documents the project needs from one set of facts. Drafts require human review. Do not send, sign, file, or collect payment unless the user separately authorizes that action.

## Establish the terms

Read the brief, approved scope, client correspondence, supplied forms, and any company-approved templates. Record the parties, project, jurisdiction, deliverables and versions, schedule, revisions, fees, expenses, payment milestones, ownership, licensed uses, and approvers. Mark each term as confirmed, proposed, or unresolved. Ask for missing information when guessing would change a price, obligation, permission, or due date.

Choose only the documents needed now. For a full project package, consider an estimate, scope of work or agreement draft, rights schedule, release checklist, and payment schedule. Create a change order or invoice when the project stage calls for one.

Prefer a user-supplied or counsel-approved agreement template. If none exists, produce a clearly labeled working draft or term sheet with unresolved terms visible; do not present it as ready to sign. Do not invent tax rules, statutory language, or permissions. Check current authoritative sources when a jurisdiction-specific rule matters, and flag it for a qualified professional.

## Estimates, agreements, and changes

For an estimate or scope, capture the objective, included deliverables and versions, technical formats, milestones, client inputs, review rounds, price basis, expenses, exclusions, and assumptions. Match deliverables to the current creative and execution plan. Do not treat a proposed option as included work.

Carry confirmed scope and commercial terms into an approved agreement template. Surface missing decisions about cancellation or postponement, revisions and overages, payment timing, delivery conditions, project files and intermediate materials, confidentiality, credit, liability, dispute venue, and ownership or license of the final work. Do not choose legal positions for the user.

A change order states the original scope, exact change, effect on deliverables, schedule and price, approval owner, and whether work may begin before approval. Do not present an unapproved change as binding.

## Rights and releases

For each material or person, record the source or rights holder, exact asset or performance, who obtained permission, where the record is stored, and the permitted media, territory, term, platforms, edits, paid advertising, and exclusivity if applicable. Record restrictions and expiration dates. Use `confirmed`, `needs evidence`, `not requested`, or `out of scope` as status labels. A supplied file or verbal assurance is not proof of clearance.

Check talent and voice, music composition and recording, stock media, artwork, logos, locations, client-supplied assets, and AI-generated material when relevant. For AI uses, surface consent for a person's likeness or voice, reuse of supplied material, and any training or model-input restrictions. The release checklist is a task list, not a claim that releases have been signed.

## Billing

Use approved project rates and payment terms. Never invent a tax rate, late fee, bank account, payment link, invoice number, or purchase-order number.

An invoice needs issuer and client details, a unique invoice number, issue and due dates, project reference, line items with quantities and rates, approved expenses or change orders, currency, subtotal, verified tax treatment if applicable, total, payment instructions, and payment terms. Mark missing fields unresolved instead of filling them with plausible data.

Calculate line totals, subtotal, tax, credits, and balance with decimal-safe arithmetic. Compare the amount billed with the signed scope, payment schedule, previous invoices, and approved changes when those records are available. Otherwise label it a draft pending reconciliation.

## Cross-check before delivery

Check that party names, project name, scope, deliverable counts and formats, dates, revisions, fees, payment terms, and usage fields agree across documents. Flag missing or conflicting terms instead of silently choosing one version. Record which template and source facts informed each draft. Contracts and taxes need review by the client's lawyer or accountant where appropriate; this mode prepares drafts and issue lists, not a compliance certificate.

# Creative brief to treatment

Make a creative decision that a client and crew can react to. This skill defines the direction; [project-breakdown](../project-breakdown/SKILL.md) turns an accepted direction into an execution plan.

## Understand the job

Read supplied briefs, scripts, brand material, project assets, references, and existing client feedback. Identify the objective, intended audience, key message, deliverables, channels, budget and timing limits, and any claims that need evidence. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the concept. Distinguish client facts from your assumptions.

## Form the treatment

State one clear creative thesis: what the audience should notice, feel, or understand, and why this approach serves the brief. Describe the story or sequence, the role of the brand or product, the visual and sound direction, and the ending or call to action when relevant. Show how the idea can be made with available people, assets, time, and budget. If a reference is supplied, say which quality it informs; do not copy its distinctive execution.

Lead with one strong direction. Offer another only if it creates a meaningful choice, such as a different story structure or emotional approach. Do not pad the response with near-identical concepts. Keep the treatment at decision-making fidelity, not a shot-by-shot plan or a generation prompt.

## Handoff

Give the next stage enough to work from: working title, creative thesis, audience promise, story beats, visual and sound rules, key references and their roles, practical constraints, deliverables, and open decisions. Mark the direction as proposed or approved based on actual client input. Do not invent testimonials, product facts, permissions, or performance claims.

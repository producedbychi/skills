# Project breakdown

Translate an accepted direction into work people can execute. Use the approved treatment if one exists. If it does not, work from the supplied brief and label decisions that remain provisional.

## Break down the work

For each story beat or deliverable, identify the visual and audio material needed to make it work in the edit. Specify shots or generated segments at the level the project needs, with purpose, action, framing or camera intent, estimated duration when useful, and the source of each element. Distinguish footage to capture, client-supplied media, licensed material, and proposed AI-generated material. Do not assume any asset is cleared merely because it was supplied.

Group capture by setup when that reduces repeat work. Include coverage for transitions, inserts, alternate formats, and claims that need visual proof. Check whether the planned material can support the target runtime and all requested aspect ratios. Identify people, locations, props, products, graphics, audio, and reference images needed. Carry continuity details forward for recurring subjects, products, wardrobe, and locations.

Add dependencies and decision points that affect execution: approvals, booking, permissions, asset delivery, technical tests, and handoff to editing. Separate confirmed resources and dates from suggestions. Do not invent a budget, crew availability, access to a location, or a locked schedule.

## Deliver the plan

Use a shot table or checklist when it makes the work easier to follow. A complete handoff identifies the beat, required material, source or owner, setup or scene, format needs, dependencies, and unresolved risks. Keep the plan proportional to the job. A simple social video does not need a feature-film dossier.

Do not turn this into a model-specific prompt pack. If AI generation is part of the approved plan, record the shot intent and reference requirements for a later generation skill. If scope or deliverables change, flag the change for [project-paperwork](../project-paperwork/SKILL.md) rather than silently expanding the job.

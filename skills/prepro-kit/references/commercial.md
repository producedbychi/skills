# Commercial documents

Read [paperwork mode](paperwork.md) first for the terms ledger and authorization limits. Prefer the user's approved estimate, master agreement, or scope template. Produce the requested document in full, with unresolved decisions visible. Do not substitute a checklist for an estimate or scope draft.

## Estimate

Identify each included deliverable by quantity, duration or format, and channel when known. Break work into meaningful cost groups such as preproduction, production, post, licensing, and expenses. Use actual approved rates, units, quantities, currency, and tax treatment when verified. Show the basis for each line and subtotal, plus estimate validity or expiration only when supplied. Separate fixed, variable, allowance, and optional costs; say how overages or pass-through expenses will be approved. Do not present an unpriced assumption as included work. Include review rounds, client inputs, milestones, exclusions, and optional work where they affect price or scope.

Reconcile the estimate against the breakdown. Every required version and costly production element should appear in the price or as a named assumption. Show the arithmetic and avoid double-counting shared work across versions. If rates or quantities are missing, provide an unpriced estimate structure and ask for them. Do not fabricate a total or imply an estimate guarantees an unknown cost.

## Scope of work and agreement draft

A scope of work should let both parties recognize the same job. State parties, project, specific deliverables and file or handoff formats, included services, client inputs and deadlines, review and acceptance process, schedule dependencies, revision allowance, fees and payment milestones, expense handling, exclusions, change control, and the relationship to any master agreement. Define whether a revision changes an approved direction or merely refines it when that distinction affects scope. Mark unapproved terms as proposed. Avoid treating a treatment's optional idea as contracted scope.

For an agreement, populate a user-supplied or counsel-approved template when possible. If no template exists, create a clearly labeled working term sheet or draft, not a purported standard contract. Surface missing decisions about cancellation or postponement, revisions and overages, payment timing, delivery conditions, project files and intermediate materials, confidentiality, credit, liability, dispute venue, and ownership or license. Check whether usage rights and third-party materials in the rights schedule match the proposed grant. Do not select legal positions for the user or label the draft ready to sign.

## Change order

Identify the original approved scope and the exact requested change. Show the before-and-after effect on deliverables, dates, price, revisions, and rights, with the basis for any new cost. Identify who approves it and whether work is authorized to begin. Keep the original and revised terms distinguishable. A requested change is not approval to bill for it.

## Check before delivery

- Every priced line has a supported rate, unit, quantity, and currency.
- Totals and allowances reconcile, and likely variable costs are not hidden in a fixed quote.
- Deliverable counts, formats, versions, and review rounds agree with the creative and production plan.
- The document separates included work, optional work, and unresolved assumptions.
- The client can see what must be approved before work or billing changes.

# Rights and releases

Read [paperwork mode](paperwork.md) first for the terms ledger and authorization limits. A rights schedule tracks the planned use of each asset or person and the evidence supporting that use. It does not grant rights or replace a release.

For each relevant item, record the exact asset or person, source and rights holder, intended use, platforms or media, territory, term, edits or derivatives, paid advertising, exclusivity if relevant, restrictions, evidence location, clearance owner, and status. Identify which deliverables use the item so a revised cut or new channel can be checked. Use a table or register that can be updated as evidence arrives.

Use `confirmed` only when permission evidence covers the planned use and the relevant rights holder or authorized licensor can be identified. Use `needs evidence` when the material exists but permission is unverified, `not requested` when clearance has not been sought, and `out of scope` when it will not be used. Note an expiration or restriction next to the affected item. A file from the client, an online post, a platform download, or a verbal assurance is not by itself proof of clearance. Keep the evidence or its location, not a copied assertion, in the register.

Check the categories relevant to the production: talent and voice; music composition and the particular recording as separate rights; stock media; artwork and logos; locations; client-supplied assets; and AI-generated material. For AI use, identify consent for a person's likeness or voice, input-material restrictions, and reuse permissions when applicable. Do not assume a model's output is free of third-party rights. Separate a release to depict a person or location from a license to use someone else's underlying work.

A release checklist names the person or property, release type, planned use, responsible party, deadline, and evidence storage location. Mark it pending until the signed or otherwise valid record is actually available. For minors or sensitive locations, flag the need for project-specific review; do not draft jurisdiction-specific consent language from memory. If a use changes, check whether the existing permission still covers it.

Before delivery, verify that every item required by the treatment or breakdown has a rights entry, and that each `confirmed` entry has matching evidence for the stated media, term, territory, and edits. Surface the items that could block capture, publication, paid distribution, or later reuse, with an owner and due date when known. Refer jurisdiction-specific release language and uncertain ownership to qualified counsel.

# Everyday products: Instacart's “Summer Like It's 1999”

[See the photographed scenes and campaign toolkit in Figma's case study](https://www.figma.com/blog/the-anatomy-of-instacart-summer-99-campaign/).

**Offer and visual mechanism.** The product is ordinary grocery fare, but the campaign stages it in playful summer moments: a snack by a playground slide, food on a tennis court, treats on a beach. Low angles, fish-eye perspective, and a shared visual system make the items feel part of a remembered experience. The products have a role in each scene rather than being pasted beside the headline.

**Adapt when:** the product's appeal is tied to an occasion, routine, or sensory moment. Choose one moment the buyer recognizes and preserve the product's real form and label. The art direction can change completely; the transferable decision is the specific use context.

**Requires:** accurate product assets and appropriate partner permissions. Do not imitate the campaign's 1990s styling unless it suits the user's brand and brief. The case study explains the production choices and mentions organic reaction, but does not give isolated conversion data for the pictured ad units.

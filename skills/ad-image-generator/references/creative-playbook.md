# Creative playbook for static ads

This is a set of decision aids, not a gallery of layouts to copy. Public ad libraries show creative, but usually not the conversion outcome for that exact image. Treat first-party results, when available, as more relevant than these cross-brand findings. Do not require first-party results to proceed.

## What the evidence supports

| Finding | How to use it | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In Meta, Kantar and CreativeX modeled 56,984 assets across 1,295 campaigns. Human presence or eye contact was associated with 81% higher effectiveness; a single visible face was the strongest tested feature. [Study summary](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/company-news/the-new-era-of-storytelling) | Consider a relevant person when the human relationship helps explain or trust the offer. | The analysis mixed images and videos in consumer categories. It did not prove that every service ad needs a face or that a static face causes conversions. |
| Ekimetrics analyzed creative features and ROI for four Meta advertisers, including hospitality and insurance. People and faces mattered in these service-like categories. [Study](https://www.iabhongkong.com/sites/default/files/2023-08/Ekimetrics_Meta_Creative_Effectiveness.pdf) | Consider a provider or customer in a real service setting when a physical product is absent. | Four-brand modeled analysis; the authors flag the intangible-offer finding for further validation. |
| An eye-tracking study of 1,363 print ads found pictures drew attention and brand elements helped move attention to other ad elements. [Pieters and Wedel, Journal of Marketing](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1509/jmkg.68.2.36.27794) | Give the image a clear job and make the brand identifiable within the composition. | Print attention is not paid-social conversion. |
| A controlled banner study found a static face alone did not increase attention, but a face looking toward the advertised element directed more viewers to it. [Palcu et al., Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00881/full) | If a person is present, use their gaze or action to point toward the meaningful part of the ad. | Small desktop banner experiment for perfume, not a universal social-feed rule. |
| System1 and JCDecaux tested static outdoor ads at brief exposure. Faster brand recognition was associated with greater campaign recall, and one clear message beat three for key brand association. [Double Take research](https://system1group.com/out-of-home) | Use a quick-glance test for one message and recognizable branding. | Outdoor recall and brand lift are not a prediction of feed-ad conversion. |

These findings agree on a practical starting point: make one relevant thing easy to notice, connect it to the advertiser, and make the promised value concrete. None identifies a universal winning layout. Apply the evidence to the user's brand and objective rather than scoring ads by a fixed formula.

## Turn an offer into a visual anchor

| Offer | Strong candidates | Weak shortcut to challenge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical product | The real item in use, its defining detail, a true comparison, or the result of using it | A floating pack that says little about why it matters |
| Creative or professional service | A real finished work sample, a person delivering the service, a concrete deliverable, or a true transformation | Generic equipment, a conference-room stock photo, or invented results |
| Software or digital service | An accurate interface, a visible workflow, a customer task, or a real output | A fabricated dashboard with impressive numbers |
| Local service | The provider, place, work, or result in its actual setting | A generic portrait that could advertise any business |
| Event, education, or community | A real participation moment, useful artifact, or credible outcome | An invented crowd, endorsement, or emotional reaction |

The best anchor may be a supplied photo or screenshot. Generate only what improves the concept. For a service with no product, the work itself can be the hero. A symbolic scene is an option when literal evidence is unavailable, but keep the offer intelligible and do not dress a symbol up as proof.

## Compare concepts without template roulette

For each possible image, ask: What does the viewer notice first? Which offer or benefit does that communicate? What makes it belong to this brand? What evidence supports the claim? Would the message survive at feed size and in the platform's actual layout?

Choose the concept that answers those questions most clearly. Visual beauty matters, but it cannot rescue an unclear offer. A public example is inspiration, not proof of conversion. Do not infer success from an ad's apparent age, repetition, likes, or spend. If the user later supplies results for their own ads, compare the exact rendered creatives against the business outcome, not click-through rate alone.

# Ad image generator

Make a static ad that communicates a specific offer and looks native to its placement. A polished image is not a proven converter. Use the built-in evidence to make better creative decisions, then judge performance only from real campaign results.

## Use the brief already available

Read the request, earlier chat, project files, and supplied brand assets. Distinguish assets actually inspected from assets only described by the user. If reviews, sales notes, search terms, comments, or past ads are available, look for repeated buyer language and objections; do not treat an isolated quote as a general insight. Do not restart a strategy interview, require past ad data, or run competitor research for an ordinary image request. If one missing decision would substantially change the image, ask one focused question. Otherwise proceed with a stated assumption. If a brand palette is described but not supplied, derive it from verified assets when possible or mark the colors provisional.

For a new concept, read [the creative playbook](references/creative-playbook.md). Read [the platform guide](references/platform-guidance.md) for the requested placement. Read [the visual examples](references/visual-examples.md) when choosing a route for an offer without an obvious image, or when the first layout feels generic. Before rendering, read the relevant part of [the production guide](references/production-guidance.md) for the available tool or prompt-only handoff. For a narrow edit, use only the material needed to preserve the approved direction. Check current official specifications when preparing final files, since ad products and limits change.

Use this short decision card internally before rendering:

- **Buyer moment:** Who sees the ad, how familiar are they with the offer, and what matters to them now?
- **Offer and action:** What is being offered, and what should the viewer do?
- **Supportable message:** What one claim can the brand actually make? What supplied fact or asset supports it?
- **Visual anchor:** What can the viewer see that makes the offer or result concrete?
- **Brand cue:** What makes this recognizable as this brand rather than a category ad?
- **Placement:** Is the image a finished layout or an asset that the platform will combine with separate text and logos?

If another skill or the user supplied the strategy and copy, preserve them. Improve how they are shown. Do not silently change the offer, audience, claim, or approved wording. If the copy is too long or visually weak, propose a specific shorter treatment and make the tradeoff clear.

## Choose the visual idea

Start with the claim and its proof. Choose one route from the creative playbook that fits the buyer's familiarity, the available assets, and the placement. For a product, that may be the real product in use, a decisive detail, or a truthful comparison. For a service, use the actual work, deliverable, provider in context, customer situation, process, or visible result. A metaphor can work when its meaning is immediate and it does not impersonate evidence. A generic camera, laptop, stock portrait, or floating product package is not a visual anchor by itself.

Use real supplied assets when identity matters. Do not invent client work, testimonials, metrics, product features, interface screens, or before-and-after results. If no real people or work samples are available, a generated scene may illustrate the category, but it must not imply that the people are actual staff or clients or that the scene documents this brand's work. A graphic or conceptual treatment may be more honest. Pick one lead concept. Make an alternative only when it tests a genuinely different visual idea or the user asks for options. Variations of the same layout are revisions, not new concepts.

Before rendering, settle the art direction: the focal subject, first and second read, crop, contrast, brand colors, exact assets, and type treatment if the image contains text. Check that the image and headline address the same buyer and make the same promise. Decide what belongs inside the image and what belongs in the platform's headline, description, logo, or button fields. Short image text is useful when it adds a message the visual cannot carry alone. Do not make text mandatory or ban it by default.

## Produce the ad

Use the image or editing tools available in the user's environment. Work from a supplied photograph or design when it carries the idea better than a new render. Follow the chosen tool's actual reference-image and edit capabilities; do not assume it can keep lettering or a package exact. For an exact logo, product, person, quote, UI, or headline, preserve the original asset or apply reliable compositing. Do not rely on generated approximations for details that must match.

If there is no image tool, or the user wants another generator, deliver a prompt package with the decision card, one paste-ready prompt, an ordered reference-image manifest, intended placement, exact text or separate platform fields, and a finish checklist. Mark missing reference files as required inputs, not as assets already verified. For precise lettering, plan a text-free base image and a separate typesetting step unless the chosen tool reliably renders exact text. Clearly distinguish a prompt package from a rendered ad. Do not switch to a different provider without the user's approval.

## Review before delivery

Inspect the rendered ad at full size, at realistic feed size, and in the platform preview when available. Check each requested crop:

- The complete ad makes the one message and offer understandable without requiring a long caption. For a responsive image asset, judge this in the assembled platform preview, not the image alone. The image, headline, and CTA address the same buyer.
- A viewer can identify the advertiser in the complete placement; the ad does not look like a competitor's or a generic category image.
- The claim, proof, product, people, typography, and logo are accurate. Text is spelled correctly.
- The image fits the platform's asset role, safe area, and policy. It does not draw a fake button or obscure required information.
- The ad and its landing destination, if known, promise the same thing.

If a check fails, make a focused repair and inspect again. If the available tools cannot fix a material error, describe it plainly rather than calling the file ready. Deliver one finished lead ad or prompt package, the target placement, and a short explanation of the creative hypothesis. If the user later supplies results, compare the exact creative, placement, audience, and business outcome, then include a brief learning note in the next deliverable. Do not claim it will convert or that it outperforms untested alternatives.

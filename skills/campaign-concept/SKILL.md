# Campaign concept

Turn approved strategy into a buyer-relevant creative idea teams can execute across requested touchpoints. Keep the insight, strategic promise, concept, execution device, and claim support distinct.

Use this after a strategic direction is approved. It develops the concept, not positioning, full copy, media plans, or rendered assets.

## 1. Set the brief

Read the request, conversation, supplied files, approved positioning, and brand assets. Establish:

- The campaign objective and the change it should support.
- The audience or buying situation the approved strategy covers.
- The exact offer, its current capabilities, terms, and limits.
- The requested touchpoints and any placement, timing, or production constraints.
- Customer evidence, claim support, and assets available for use.

An approved direction sets a boundary; it does not prove claims or audience assumptions. Label audience statements observed, supplied, or hypothetical, and name sources. One quote speaks for its author. Public comments and competitor material suggest questions, not customer evidence.

Ask one focused question if a missing fact changes the audience, offer, promise, or objective. For a quick idea, use a labeled assumption when safe. Keep unconfirmed outcomes and capabilities provisional.

Complete this step when the strategic direction, actual offer, objective, requested scope, and evidence boundaries are clear or explicitly provisional.

## 2. Find the campaign idea

Start with the buyer's relevant situation, want, friction, or decision. Use available evidence. For functional decisions, do not manufacture pain or emotion. Connect offer attributes to supportable consequences. Check actual alternatives when choice matters to the objective.

When direction is open, compare concepts that preserve the approved strategic promise while varying what the audience recognizes or how the offer demonstrates value. Different wording or styling alone is not a new concept. Develop user-approved ideas directly.

Compare concepts for positioning fit, buyer relevance, offer clarity, memorable brand-linked execution, support, and feasibility.

With performance data, name the strongest tested angle for this objective, noting audience, offer, placement, or version differences. Use it as a baseline. A new concept may lead for a concrete strategic, customer-evidence, or production reason. If it does, state why it should lead now, the challenger hypothesis, and what observed result versus baseline would justify keeping it. Past results are evidence, not guarantees.

Note serious weaknesses and choose the strongest fit. Show alternatives only when requested or when a tradeoff needs a decision. Do not invent scores or claim conversion gains.

Keep these parts distinct in the working brief:

- **Insight:** What the buyer wants, notices, believes, does, or compares in a specific situation. Give its evidence label and source. A hypothesis remains unproven.
- **Strategic promise:** The relevant value the actual offer can credibly provide. Separate current delivery from proposed changes. A customer desire does not prove fulfillment.
- **Creative concept:** The campaign's central idea, expressed in a short sentence that connects the insight to the promise and gives the viewer something to see, hear, compare, or discover. A benefit restated in new words is not yet an organizing idea. A useful technical distinction can lead directly when it fits the buyer and strategy.
- **Execution device:** A repeatable visible or verbal action, contrast, reveal, phrase, or relationship that makes the concept recognizable and ties it to the brand through approved assets, language, or behavior. Explain how it adapts to each format. Do not force a metaphor or visual motif. The device is a design choice, not claim evidence.
- **Claim support:** The supplied fact, authentic evidence asset, or qualification for each factual assertion. Mark gaps. An illustrative scene cannot establish results, testimonials, credentials, or product capabilities.

Complete this step when the concept links a sourced or labeled insight to a deliverable promise, gives the audience a concrete organizing idea, and has a recognizable, feasible device with supported claims.

## 3. Show how it works

Write a brief another person can execute. Name the idea only if useful. State the audience situation, insight and evidence, promise, concept, device, and fit with the objective.

For each requested touchpoint, describe the encounter, how the device and offer work in that format, the viewer's next action, and supporting assets or claims. A static ad may show a contrast; video may reveal it through action; a landing-page opening may connect it to the offer. When asked for execution detail, include useful on-screen words or video beats at concept level, not a full script unless requested. Use only requested formats and no fixed layout or format count.

Keep examples at concept level unless the user asks for copy. Label sample words as draft language. Tie prices, outcomes, comparisons, guarantees, client results, interfaces, testimonials, credentials, and deadlines to supplied support. Describe missing support as a production dependency. Do not promise improved results.

Complete this step when each requested execution names the audience's encounter, its connection to the idea, the identifiable offer, the next action, and the assets or production needs that make it feasible.

## 4. Check and hand off

Check the brief against its sources and executions:

- The audience situation and objective follow from the approved strategy.
- The concept makes sense for the actual offer and available delivery.
- The touchpoints share one idea while doing useful work in their own formats.
- The execution device is recognizable and feasible with the supplied assets or stated production needs.
- Every audience-facing factual claim has support or a qualification visible with it, and every open assumption is labeled. An internal caveat cannot repair misleading draft copy or imagery.
- The chosen concept is stronger for this brief than the serious alternatives considered.

Fix mismatches before delivery. If positioning conflicts with the offer or evidence, state the conflict and give the best provisional route within the brief. Ask for a decision only if it changes the concept materially.

Deliver the selected concept, requested executions, and concise notes on assumptions, assets, or claim support. Include alternatives only when requested or decision-relevant. Keep discarded concepts internal unless asked.

For downstream production, include a self-contained handoff with the audience, situation, concept, device, approved wording, supported claims, assets, and open dependencies. Mark recommendations. A handoff does not mean copy, video, landing-page, image, or performance work is complete.

Complete the assignment when the brief passes those checks, contains the requested adaptations, and separates current facts from proposals and unresolved dependencies. Match the output length and format to the request.

# Archetype router

Identify which archetype the scene fits. The archetype guides camera behavior, spatial logic, and what changes across time. This is independent of genre — you can have a horror Reveal, an action Reveal, or a commercial Reveal, and they all share the same camera grammar even though their vocabulary is different.

---

## Action archetypes

### Pursuit

**Camera focus:** Distance closing or opening between pursuer and pursued. The pursued reads ahead in frame; the pursuer reads behind.

**Space dynamic:** Path narrows or opens. Compression and release as the chase moves through environments.

**Camera signature:**
- Lateral tracking parallel to the chase
- Behind-the-pursued OTS or behind-the-pursuer OTS (alternate)
- Wide establishing of the gap (showing distance)
- Body-mount on either runner or vehicle for visceral close-up
- Speed ramps on impact moments

**Time dynamic:** Either the gap closes (pursuer wins, dramatic tension peaks) or the gap opens (pursued escapes, dramatic tension releases).

**When to use:** Chase scenes, cat-and-mouse, fleeing, hunting, racing.

---

### Duel

**Camera focus:** Camera lower on the dominant side of the exchange. Dominance MUST alternate — neither side dominates more than one consecutive beat.

**Space dynamic:** Fighters trade position. Whoever's winning is upstage / lower-angle; whoever's losing is downstage / higher-angle.

**Camera signature:**
- Low-angle on the dominant fighter
- High-angle or eye-level on the losing fighter (subordinate)
- Quick cuts between the two as advantage trades
- 180° rule maintained until a beat where the camera deliberately crosses the axis (signaling power flip)
- Reaction inserts (bruising, weapon ready) between exchanges

**Time dynamic:** Power oscillates. Each beat is a small win for one side, then a counter from the other.

**Rule:** If one fighter dominates the whole scene without giving up ground, describe it as a one-sided assault rather than a duel with alternating advantage. Duels require trade.

**When to use:** Two-character fights, sword duels, MMA, rap battles, debate, tense card games.

---

### Impact

**Camera focus:** Build-up slow → contact fast → aftermath slow. The point of contact is the geometric center of the entire scene's composition.

**Space dynamic:** Both subjects converge on a single moment, then spatial energy releases outward.

**Camera signature:**
- Slow push-in or stable lateral on the build-up
- Cut to extreme close-up macro on the impact frame, often slow-motion
- Pull-back on the aftermath, revealing what the impact did to the environment
- Multiple cuts on the impact moment (different angles of the same hit)

**Time dynamic:** Three-act timing — Long → Short (impact) → Long. Build-up and aftermath are roughly equal in duration; the impact moment is much shorter.

**When to use:** Single decisive hit, single decisive moment of contact, plot turning points, "bullet through the air" beats, dramatic reveals at moment of contact.

---

### Action decision tree

1. Someone chasing or being chased? → **Pursuit**
2. Two opponents trading dominance? → **Duel**
3. Single decisive moment of contact? → **Impact**
4. None of the above clearly fits → default to **Duel**

## General archetypes

### Journey

**Camera focus:** Position in space. The subject moves through environments and the camera tracks the movement.

**Space dynamic:** Environment changes around the subject. Landscapes pass. Architecture shifts.

**Camera signature:**
- Lateral tracking shots (most common)
- Aerial / drone descents into the journey
- Body-mount or vehicle-mount for first-person feel
- Hard cuts between environmental segments
- Pull-back reveals at scope-changing beats

**Time dynamic:** Linear progression through space. Beginning → middle → destination.

**When to use:** Road trips, walks, flights, processions, paths through environments.

---

### Atmosphere

**Camera focus:** Nothing — mood IS the content. The drama is in light, weather, micro-changes within a fixed environment.

**Space dynamic:** Subject doesn't move significantly. Environment doesn't change significantly. The shot lives on tiny shifts: a breeze through curtains, rain against glass, candlelight flickering.

**Camera signature:**
- Minimal movement — slow push-in or static hold
- Rack-focus shifts as the only "movement"
- Long takes on the same composition
- Macro inserts on textural details

**Time dynamic:** Minimal external change. Internal nuance carries the beat.

**When to use:** Establishing shots, mood-only scenes, contemplative moments, before-the-action establishment, after-the-action denouement.

---

### Reveal

**Camera focus:** Hidden → visible. The camera controls WHEN the viewer sees the subject.

**Space dynamic:** Subject is occluded at the start (by environment, by camera angle, by darkness, by smoke) and revealed by camera movement, by the subject moving into frame, or by the environment clearing.

**Camera signature:**
- Pan that ends on the subject
- Crane that descends to the subject
- Pull-back from a detail to a wider context
- Push-in through occlusion (a doorway, a curtain, fog) to the subject
- Match cut where the previous shot's subject becomes the next shot's container

**Time dynamic:** Hidden → mid-reveal → revealed. The reveal beat is where the dramatic peak lives.

**When to use:** Establishing the hero, dramatic entrances, twist reveals, product hero shots, environment establishments.

---

### General decision tree

1. Subject moves through space, position changing? → **Journey**
2. Something hidden becomes visible? → **Reveal**
3. Nothing changes — mood IS the content? → **Atmosphere**
4. None of the above clearly fits → default to **Atmosphere**

## Dialogue archetypes

### Confrontation

**Power dynamic:** Shifting — both characters push. Dominance trades per exchange.

**Camera signature:**
- Tight OTS on whoever is currently speaking
- Camera crosses axis at the power-shift beat (signals the flip)
- Cuts on the power-shift line (the line where dominance changes)
- Reaction inserts on the listener
- Wider two-shot at the start to establish, tighter as the confrontation peaks

**When to use:** Arguments, accusations, breakups, public callouts, executive showdowns.

---

### Interrogation

**Power dynamic:** Asymmetric — one extracts, one resists.

**Camera signature:**
- Low-angle on the questioner (dominant)
- Eye-level or slight high-angle on the subject (subordinate)
- Push-in on the subject during silences (pressure)
- Pull-back on the questioner during reveals (control)
- Locked-off wide as a recurring composition (clinical)

**When to use:** Police interrogations, deposition scenes, parent-child confrontation, doctor-patient where the doctor is extracting.

---

### Negotiation

**Power dynamic:** Balanced — both characters need something from the other.

**Camera signature:**
- Symmetrical framing — matching shot sizes on both characters
- Even cut rhythm between the two
- Two-shot establishes equality
- Inserts on subtle physical tells (a hand drumming, a glance away, a shift in the chair)

**When to use:** Business deals, romantic stand-offs, two-party negotiations, partnerships forming, treaties.

---

### Dialogue decision tree

1. Both characters pushing, dominance trading? → **Confrontation**
2. One extracting, one resisting? → **Interrogation**
3. Both need something, balanced? → **Negotiation**
4. None of the above → default to **Confrontation**

## Dialogue word limit

Roughly 25–30 spoken words fit into 15 seconds of video. If the user provides more dialogue than that:

- Keep the **power-shift exchange** (the line where dominance flips or truth emerges)
- Keep 1 line before (setup)
- Keep 1 line after (reaction)
- Convert everything else to physical behavior — beats of action that bridge the cut dialogue

Example. User provides 8 lines, totaling 80 words. Keep the line where she says "I'm leaving." Keep the line he says before ("after everything I—"). Keep her reaction line ("watch me."). Convert the other 5 lines into physical beats: she pulls her ring off, sets it on the counter, picks up her bag, opens the door, doesn't look back.

## Hybrid scenes

Scenes can be hybrids. A pursuit can include impact moments. A confrontation can become a duel. A reveal can be the first beat of a journey.

When a scene is hybrid, identify the dominant archetype (the one that takes the most runtime) and use its camera signature as the spine. Then borrow camera moves from the secondary archetype at the moments it surfaces.

Example: Hayden Valley Scene 1 is a hybrid. The first 2 seconds are dialogue-adjacent (POV + reverse on the buyer = a one-sided emotional confrontation). The next 5 seconds are a Reveal (cluttered desk hidden → product cascade revealed). Dominant: Reveal. Borrow: Confrontation OTS framing for the first 2 seconds.

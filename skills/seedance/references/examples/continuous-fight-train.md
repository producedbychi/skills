# Continuous example: Train Fight

Two female samurai fight on top of a speeding train. The camera crests the rooftop edge, orbits both fighters mid-clash, ramps into slow motion for a single hair-severing dodge, then follows both warriors as they fall off the carriage simultaneously into the river below — and continues underwater. Single continuous shot, 15 seconds.

This example demonstrates: continuous format with timestamped beats, camera evolution within a single shot (orbit, slow-motion ramp, fall, plunge), no cuts despite multiple environmental changes (rooftop → air → water).

**Genre:** action-intense + cinematic-narrative (composed)
**Format:** continuous
**Capability:** consistency-control (image references for both warriors)

---

## The prompt

```
Single continuous shot 15s: The camera begins below the train roof level — an extreme low angle looking up from beneath the rushing steel edge, the grey sky above and the train's metal roof surface filling the upper frame, power line cables visible on both sides, electric sparks already arcing between the cables and the roof surface in irregular white-blue bursts. The FPV arm accelerates upward and crests the rooftop edge just as both female samurai asian girls warriors sprint into frame from opposite ends of the carriage roof — dark armor catching the electric flash light, katanas already drawn and trailing behind them — the camera dropping to roof level and sweeping immediately into a full 360-degree orbit around both fighters as they clash center-roof, blades ringing against each other, feet sliding on the curved metal surface, the passing landscape blurring at speed on both sides and the electric sparks erupting in curtains around them with each power cable they pass beneath.

The orbit completes and the camera settles tight behind the first warrior as she draws back her katana in a full overhead swing — the motion ramping into deep slow motion, every detail suspended: the blade arc, the electric spark frozen mid-burst beside her shoulder, the second warrior already reading the strike and arching her entire body backward in a deep spine bend, the katana blade sweeping through the space where her face was a fraction of a second before, the flat of the blade passing directly in front of her eyes at centimeter distance — and a single small lock of dark hair catching the blade edge and separating, the severed strand drifting upward in the slow motion wind.

The motion snaps back to full speed as the second warrior completes her dodge and drives her elbow into the first warrior's chest simultaneously, the two fighters exchanging rapid blade-locked strikes across the carriage roof as the electric sparks shower around them. The camera pulls back to a wide rooftop angle as both warriors simultaneously lock arms, pivot, and drive each other outward over the edge — both bodies leaving the roof in the same moment, tumbling sideways off the carriage and falling through open air toward the river running parallel to the track below, the camera descending with them in a controlled drop, both fighters still gripping their katanas with arms extended toward each other during the two-second fall. Both bodies hit the river surface simultaneously in twin white explosions, and the camera plunges beneath the surface with them — the world going deep blue-green, sunlight filtering down in shafts from above, both warriors already moving through the underwater space toward each other, dark armor trailing bubbles, katanas extended, the fight continuing in the silent slow drift of the river current.

NO MUSIC / NO SFX

Total: 15s / 1 shot / 16:9
```

---

## Why this prompt works

**Continuous format with massive environmental change.** The camera goes from below-the-train, to on-the-rooftop, into-the-air, into-water — all in one shot, no cuts. Most prompts would fight this. This one explicitly states "Single continuous shot 15s" at the top and never breaks the camera.

**Camera evolution as the structural device.** The camera does not move arbitrarily. It evolves through the scene:
1. Below roof level (extreme low angle looking up)
2. Crest the rooftop edge (FPV upward)
3. Drop to roof level
4. Full 360-degree orbit around the fighters
5. Settle tight behind first warrior
6. Slow-motion ramp during the strike
7. Snap back during the elbow drive
8. Pull back to wide rooftop
9. Descend with the falling fighters
10. Plunge beneath the water surface

Each beat has its own camera character, but the camera never cuts.

**The slow-motion ramp is the dramatic peak.** The hair-severing dodge happens in slow-motion — the centimeter-distance miss, the severed strand drifting upward. This is the moment the audience remembers. One ramp, perfectly placed.

**Sensory specificity.** "Electric sparks already arcing... in irregular white-blue bursts" — specific. "Single small lock of dark hair catching the blade edge and separating" — specific. "Sunlight filtering down in shafts from above" — specific. Every visual detail Seedance can render is named.

**"NO MUSIC / NO SFX"** — explicit silence direction. The fight happens in real-world ambient (sparks, train, water) without scoring. This is a deliberate choice — silent action reads as more brutal.

---

## What to learn from this prompt

- **Continuous format CAN handle environmental changes.** The trick is letting the camera move physically through them rather than cutting.
- **The 360-orbit is a continuous-format signature beat.** It gives multiple angles without cuts. Use sparingly — overuse becomes gimmicky.
- **Specific sensory anchors.** "Single small lock of dark hair," "electric flash light catching dark armor," "sunlight filtering down in shafts" — these are the details the audience locks onto. Pack the prompt with them.
- **Slow-motion ramps work in continuous format.** "The motion ramping into deep slow motion... The motion snaps back to full speed." Same speed-change rules as multi-shot, applied to continuous.
- **Negation can be silent.** "NO MUSIC / NO SFX" tells Seedance the audio register. Sometimes explicit silence is the right call.

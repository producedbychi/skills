# Composed example: Cinematic commercial with high action

The buyer at a Hayden Valley sample-case desk experiences product as a kinetic transformation — the case OPEN, the assortment ERUPTING outward in cinematic slow-motion, the buyer's face shifting from skeptical to settled relief. Demonstrates how three genres compose: commercial-product (base) + cinematic-narrative (modifier) + action-intense (modifier).

This example shows: how the same brief that would normally read as "static product reveal" becomes kinetic-cinematic when the action-intense modifier is applied, while still keeping the product as hero (commercial-product base).

**Genre:** commercial-product (base) + cinematic-narrative (modifier) + action-intense (modifier)
**Format:** multishot
**Capability:** consistency-control (buyer character reference + product reference)

---

## The prompt

```
Hyper-cinematic commercial action realism, ARRI ALEXA Mini, anamorphic 35mm at f/2 for character and 100mm Macro at f/4 for product detail, mixed controlled handheld and locked-off, Snyder-style speed-ramping, premium color grade with deep preserved shadows and warm Kodak Portra warmth on skin, halation on highlights, fine grain, 2.35:1 widescreen, 24fps.

The buyer @image1 sits across from a closed Hayden Valley kraft corrugated bulk case @image2 in a clean modern office workspace, warm overhead diffusion catching dust drifting in the air. The case face is held clean for label composite. The desk is warm-stained polished oak, a single notepad and pen at the far edge.

Shot 1: Medium close-up locked-off on the buyer @image1, eyes flicking down toward the case across the desk. Slight skepticism in the brow. She lifts her hand, pulls the case top toward her — the kraft flaps unfurling in a controlled motion, edges crisp.

Shot 2: Cut to extreme close-up macro 100mm — the case interior REVEALED. The first dark chocolate-enrobed almond cluster CATCHES a long specular highlight as the flap rises, surface tempered to mirror finish, the cluster TIPPING outward toward camera. RAMPS TO SLOW MOTION as the cluster begins its fall.

Shot 3: Cut to wide low-angle handheld — the assortment ERUPTING from the case mouth across the warm-stained oak desk in slow-motion: the dark chocolate cluster TUMBLING twice across the wood, the Pickup Cup of Blueberry Yogurt Pretzels SPILLING pale-lavender pretzels in a fanned arc, the Mixed Nuts and Sesame Sticks bowl tipping forward and POURING its contents in a wave, Strawberry Yogurt Pretzels SCATTERING across the surface, Caramel Corn kernels TUMBLING outward in glossy amber bursts, Whole Natural Almonds RELEASED from their burlap sack and rolling. SNAPS BACK to real-time at the moment the assortment settles into its final composition.

Shot 4: Cut to medium close-up on the buyer's face — the same composition as Shot 1 but the expression has TRANSFORMED. Brow softens, corners of the mouth lift in a quiet half-smile, breath releases through the nose, shoulders drop in stages. She doesn't speak. The hand resting at her temple in Shot 1 has come down to rest open on the desk.

Shot 5: Cut to a wide locked-off three-quarter overhead of the final assortment composition on the desk — eight stations of Hayden Valley product spread across the warm-stained oak, the buyer's relaxed hand visible at the lower edge of frame, the case face clean for end-card composite, the upper third of the frame held as clean negative space for closing copy lockup.

SFX: ambient office tone with distant phone ringing, single soft kraft-paper unfurl, slow-motion stretched cluster fall and contact with wood, slow-motion product cascade across the surface, snap-back to real-time as the assortment settles, single soft exhale from the buyer, calm room tone holding through the close.

Negative: no watermarks, no subtitles, no on-screen text overlays, no logos visible on the case face (held clean for composite).

Total: 15s / 5 shots / 16:9
```

---

## Why this prompt composes well

**Commercial-product base preserves structure.** The product is the hero. The case opens, the assortment is revealed, the buyer's response is to the product. The end frame is the assortment with negative space for end-card. All commercial-product structural priorities held.

**Action-intense modifier provides verb density.** ERUPTING, TUMBLING, SPILLING, POURING, SCATTERING, RELEASED — these are Tier 4 action verbs applied to product behavior. The cluster doesn't "settle" — it TUMBLES. The pretzels don't "fall" — they SCATTER. The energy register is high without a fight scene.

**Cinematic-narrative modifier provides craft.** Camera grammar is controlled (locked-off Shot 1, handheld Shot 3, locked-off Shot 5). The buyer's emotional arc lives in the face beats (Shot 1 skepticism → Shot 4 transformation). The held resolution beat (Shot 5) is cinematic, not commercial-bright. Color grade is Kodak Portra warmth, deep shadows preserved — film-stock register, not commercial-clean.

**Slow-motion is placed deliberately.** ONE ramp, at the climactic product release (Shot 2 → Shot 3), with explicit SNAPS BACK. This is action-intense's signature, applied to a commercial moment.

**The expression transformation is the cinematic-narrative signature.** Shot 4's face beat — "Brow softens, corners of the mouth lift in a quiet half-smile, breath releases through the nose, shoulders drop in stages" — is transformation language, not state language. This is what cinematic-narrative does to a commercial.

**Negative space held for composite.** Production-grade prompt — case face clean for label composite, upper third of final frame clean for copy lockup, label panels reserved. Commercial-product structural rule preserved.

---

## What this composition demonstrates

- **Commercial-product can be HIGH ENERGY.** The product still wins, but it gets there kinetically. The "settle on the surface" version would be sleepy. This version is cinematic and propulsive.
- **The base structure determines the resolution beat.** Even with action-intense and cinematic-narrative composing, the resolution is still product-centric (Shot 5 is the assortment composition with end-card negative space). This is what "base wins on STRUCTURE" means.
- **The modifier vocabulary determines verb energy.** Action-intense gives us ERUPTING, TUMBLING, etc. Cinematic-narrative gives us "softens, lifts, releases, drops in stages." The two vocabularies coexist in different beats.
- **No fight, no violence, but high action.** The kinetic energy applies to PRODUCT BEHAVIOR. The chocolate cluster tumbles, the pretzels scatter, the kernels burst — these are product motions written as action. Action-intense doesn't require combat.

---

## When to use this composition

- Trade show / B2B work that needs to stand out from the category baseline
- Premium product launches where standard commercial reads as too bland
- Hayden Valley-style brand work where the product breadth IS the message
- Any commercial where "more energy" is the brief but the product still has to be the answer

---

## When NOT to use this composition

- B2C / DTC product work targeting "trust-driven" audiences where action reads as overproduced
- Documentary brand storytelling (use commercial-product + documentary-warm instead)
- Educational / explainer commercials (use commercial-product + explainer-clarity instead)
- UGC-aesthetic ad creative (use commercial-product + ugc-authentic instead)

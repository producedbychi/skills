# Commercial / product

> **Lens note.** The camera stacks in this file are grade, stock and finish templates. Their millimeter values are legacy — convert the lens to a FOV degree from the ladder in `optics.md` before writing (`47° (50mm)`, `29° (85mm)`, `84° (24mm)`). Millimeters never lead, and apertures, ISO and lens brand names control nothing.


The product is the hero. Beauty shots, process credibility, and operational realism are the answer. Use as base genre for: B2B trade show videos, advertising, product launches, capability reels, manufacturing showcases, e-commerce product pages.

---

## Structural priorities (when used as base)

1. **Product is the resolution beat.** Every scene either reveals, demonstrates, or showcases the product. The product wins the frame at the climax.
2. **Process is credibility.** Behind-the-scenes manufacturing, hands at work, materials being transformed — these earn trust the beauty shot can't.
3. **Hero composition at the close.** Final beat is the product clean against its environment, lit to flatter, ready for end-card composite.
4. **Flat label panels held clean for composite.** When real packaging is involved, leave label faces blank in the prompt so post-production can lay branding without re-rolling generations.

---

## Verb register (default Tier 2 — Active / present)

walk, step, turn, reach, grasp, lift, tip, pour, set, place, lower, raise, push, pull, slide, roll, fold, unfold, open, close, press, tap, hold, stack, arrange, position, pivot, swivel, glance, watch, study, scan, follow, track, settle, rest, breathe, exhale.

For "high action commercial" (commercial + action-intense modifier), verbs escalate to Tier 3–4: snap, pour with force, slam, drop, scatter, tumble, cascade, burst, erupt. The product still wins; it just gets there with more energy.

---

## Camera grammar defaults

- **Establishing:** wide stabilized track or slow crane descent
- **Process beats:** medium 35–50mm with controlled handheld or stabilized lateral
- **Product detail:** 90–100mm Macro, locked-off or very slow push-in
- **Hero shot:** 50mm at f/4–5.6, slight overhead three-quarter angle
- **End beat:** locked-off, camera holds completely still for end-card composite

Avoid: hyper-chaotic handheld, dutch angles, snap zooms, whip pans (these dominate action genre — use them as accents only when commercial composes with action).

---

## Lighting defaults

- Warm overhead diffusion (north-style softbox)
- Soft warm key from one side
- Cool fill from opposite side
- Dust-free air for clean product, atmospheric particulate (cocoa fines, sugar haze, sesame dust) for process credibility
- Specular highlight bloom on glossy surfaces
- Long highlight rolloff on metal

---

## Color grade

- Warm Golden Hour with Kodak Portra warmth in wood
- Clean true blacks in chocolate / dark surfaces
- Controlled highlights — no clipping
- Editorial cool-neutral on stainless steel and modern industrial environments
- Industrial Noir desaturation for "vendor sprawl" / pain-state contrast beats

---

## Atmospheric vocabulary

Approved adjectives that land for product / process work:

- Warm-stained white oak (consistent surface vocabulary)
- Brushed pewter / brushed stainless (industrial materials)
- Slate-blue linen, slate-blue wardrobe (consistent fabric vocabulary)
- Cocoa-haze, sugar-haze, sesame dust (process particulate)
- Heat-haze (roasting, tempering)
- Mahogany-to-near-black gradient (chocolate)
- Pale-lavender, warm pink-rose, burnt-orange (yogurt-coat flavor colors)
- Honey patina, satin micro-texture (material finishes)
- Specular highlight, halation on highlights (light quality)

---

## What this genre does NOT do

- Anime panic expressions, slow-motion bullet shots, demon cauldrons
- Hyper-chaotic handheld camera (unless modifier applied)
- Cataclysmic Tier 5 verbs (DETONATES, ANNIHILATES, OBLITERATES) — unless action-intense modifier is composed
- VFX-driven climaxes (the product IS the climax, not energy effects)

---

## Composition with action-intense modifier

When the user wants "high action commercial" or "intense product video," compose commercial-product (base) with action-intense (modifier).

What changes:
- Verbs escalate from Tier 2 to Tier 3–4
- Camera grammar adds snap cuts, faster lateral tracks, dutch angles as accents
- Cut frequency increases (an 11s scene goes from 4 cuts to 6–7)
- Slow-motion punctuation on key product moments
- Atmospheric intensity: more particulate, more dramatic light raking

What stays:
- Product is still the hero
- Process and product beats still anchor the structure
- Beauty shot at the close
- Flat label panels still held clean

Example:

> The chocolate cluster doesn't "settle on the surface" — it DROPS into the assortment, surface tension SHATTERING the chocolate sheen, the cluster TUMBLING twice before settling. Camera SNAPS in, holds for half a beat, SNAPS to the wider hero composition.

The cluster still wins the frame. It just gets there kinetically.

---

## Composition with cinematic-narrative modifier

When the user wants "cinematic commercial," compose commercial-product (base) with cinematic-narrative (modifier).

What changes:
- Camera grammar gets craftful — controlled push-ins, deliberate frames, character beats around the product
- A brief character arc may live within the commercial (frustrated buyer → relieved buyer in your Hayden Valley work)
- Color grade goes deeper (more film-print quality, less commercial-clean)
- Dialogue or VO beats may anchor the scene structure

What stays:
- Product still resolves the scene
- The character serves the product, not the other way around
- Beauty hero at the close

## Common scenes

- Bulk distribution case opening, product visible spilling
- Process line: enrobing curtain, panning drum, mixing bowl
- Hero assortment: multi-station product spread on warm-stained oak
- Plant exterior: golden-hour facility, midwestern industrial
- Heritage cues: brass scale, burlap sack, pine plank table (warm Kodak Portra cast)
- Buyer outcome: relaxed face, clean desk, single product case open

These are the scenes Hayden Valley work uses. The vocabulary is consistent across them.

# Horror / thriller

> **Lens note.** The camera stacks in this file are grade, stock and finish templates. Their millimeter values are legacy — convert the lens to a FOV degree from the ladder in `optics.md` before writing (`47° (50mm)`, `29° (85mm)`, `84° (24mm)`). Millimeters never lead, and apertures, ISO and lens brand names control nothing.


Atmospheric tension, slow build, hard pressure beats. Use as base genre for: horror, psychological thriller, suspense, atmospheric dread. Use as modifier for: any genre where the user wants tension, unease, or pressure infused into the base.

---

## Structural priorities (when used as base)

1. **Tension is currency.** The scene's value is in what's about to happen, not what is happening. The reveal is paid for by the build.
2. **Restraint at the climax.** Horror tradition: implication beats explication. A held shot of an empty doorway hits harder than the monster fully seen.
3. **Atmospheric weather.** The environment carries dread — light, shadow, sound, weather all participate in the threat.
4. **Slow build.** A 15-second horror scene spends 10 seconds building and 3–5 seconds releasing (or holding back release).

## Verb register (Tier 1 → Tier 4, escalating)

**Build phase (Tier 1 / Tier 2):**
drift, settle, gather, hold, pool, breathe, hover, suspend, lean, tilt, shift, ease, fold, lift slow, lower slow, watch, hold breath, stop moving.

**Threat introduction (mid-Tier 2 / Tier 3):**
hesitate, freeze, stare, snap (head turn), grip, tighten, lurch, jerk, dart (eyes only), step backward, stumble, crash (single sound).

**Climax / release (Tier 3 / Tier 4 — sparingly):**
DETONATE, RUPTURE, ERUPT, COLLAPSE, SHATTER, BLAST.

The horror climax often holds rather than explodes. The held silence after the threat is often more effective than a Tier 5 detonation.

## Camera grammar defaults

- **Establishing:** very slow push-in, locked-off wide, or stabilized lateral
- **Build beats:** locked-off or imperceptibly drifting camera (never handheld unless the unease is part of the atmosphere)
- **Threat reveal:** rack-focus shift, crane up to reveal, slow pan across to disclose
- **Reaction beats:** medium close-up handheld with intentional micro-jitters
- **Climax / release:** snap-zoom in, fast lateral track, or HOLD on the still aftermath

The horror camera resists movement until the threat earns it. Most cuts are between locked-off compositions.

---

## Lighting defaults

- High contrast with deep crushed-but-detailed shadows
- Single hard source motivating direction (window, candle, doorway)
- Backlight / silhouette frequently
- Volumetric atmosphere (fog, dust, smoke catching light beams)
- Tyndall effect for god-rays through occlusion
- Color contrast: cool environment, warm interior practicals, or vice versa

Avoid: flat fill (kills shadow drama), bright daylight (unless the horror is daylight horror — Midsommar register), commercial-clean exposure.

---

## Atmosphere modifiers

Pile on for horror — atmosphere is dense by design.

- Fog / mist (deep, low-hanging, drifting)
- Smoke / dust (settled, suspended, agitated)
- Rain / wet surfaces (puddles, dripping, condensation)
- Volumetric beams of light through atmosphere
- Heat-haze distortion (uncanny mid-scene)
- Heavy grain (35mm film stock or 16mm grain)
- Halation bloom on practical lights
- Slight focus breathing, shallow depth dropping background into uncertainty

---

## Color grade

- Industrial Noir desaturated cool
- Crushed-but-detailed blacks
- Slight green tint in shadows (classic horror)
- Warm practicals as the only saturation
- Halation on highlights
- Heavy grain throughout

## Sound design (always include SFX block)

Horror lives in sound. The SFX block is essential.

```
SFX: ambient room tone with low-frequency rumble, distant wind through trees, single floor creak, breath holding, wet drip in deep background, sudden silence (cut all ambient), single sharp impact sound, return to ambient with new tension layer.
```

The "sudden silence" is the horror signature. Cut all ambient at the threat reveal — the absence of sound creates dread.

---

## Restraint principles

Three patterns horror does well that other genres don't:

### The held empty frame
After a beat where the audience expects something, hold the empty frame for 1–2 seconds. The threat's absence is the threat.

> Shot 4: Locked-off medium on the empty doorway. Camera holds. Wind drifts a curtain into frame. Nothing more. Hold for 2 full seconds.

### The implication insert
Show the consequence without showing the cause. A drop of blood on a white floor. A single tooth on a hardwood plank. The scratch on the wall. Audience completes the picture.

> Shot 6: Extreme close-up macro on the white floor. A single drop of dark fluid lands. Camera holds. Another drop lands beside it.

### The cut to silence
Mid-action, cut to a still frame and hold. The break in rhythm is more disturbing than the action would have been.

> Shot 3: She runs through the corridor — SMASH CUT to an empty corridor, no runner, no sound. Hold for 3 seconds. Cut to black.

## Composition with other genres

### As modifier on `cinematic-narrative`
- Adds dread to a character-driven scene
- The character's interior weather becomes the scene's atmospheric weather
- "The Lighthouse," "There Will Be Blood" composition

### As modifier on `commercial-product`
- Rare but powerful for "before vs after" beats
- The "vendor sprawl" beat in Hayden Valley Scene 1 borrows horror techniques (Industrial Noir, fluorescent dread, flat unflattering color)
- Use sparingly — full horror in commercial reads as off-tone

### As modifier on `documentary-warm`
- True crime aesthetic
- Adds tension to observational footage
- Slow build over reveal

### As modifier on `action-intense`
- Slow-build pressure that releases into kinetic action
- "The Babadook" final act, "It Follows" climax

---

## What this genre does NOT do

- Fast-cut high-energy action throughout (release moments only)
- Bright commercial lighting (ever)
- Dense verb density across all beats (build phase is sparse)
- Explicit climax explanation (implication over explication)
- Resolution that closes all questions (horror often ends ambiguously)

## Common scenes

- Empty environment with implied threat (hallway, woods, room)
- Character realizing something is wrong (face beat, no dialogue)
- Slow approach toward a closed door / dark space
- Reveal of consequence without cause (blood, scratch, displaced object)
- Held silence after a sudden noise
- Reaction insert on a face frozen in dread
- Wide environmental establishing with single off-element (figure in distance, light in window)

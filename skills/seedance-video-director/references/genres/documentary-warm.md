# Documentary / warm observational

> **Lens note.** The camera stacks in this file are grade, stock and finish templates. Their millimeter values are legacy — convert the lens to a FOV degree from the ladder in `optics.md` before writing (`47° (50mm)`, `29° (85mm)`, `84° (24mm)`). Millimeters never lead, and apertures, ISO and lens brand names control nothing.


Real-world subjects, observational camera, heritage palette. Use as base genre for: documentary content, brand heritage stories, founder profiles, behind-the-scenes, archival reconstruction, observational ad creative, "real story" content.

---

## Structural priorities (when used as base)

1. **Observational, not directed.** The camera follows what's happening rather than making it happen. Subjects act naturally; camera captures.
2. **Heritage palette.** Warm Kodak Portra cast, fine grain, halation on highlights. Suggests film over digital.
3. **Restraint.** No theatrical performance, no choreographed action, no on-the-nose dialogue. The drama is in the real moment.
4. **Sense of place.** Environments are specific, lived-in, not staged. Wardrobe is worn, not styled. Surfaces show use.

## Verb register (Tier 1 / Tier 2 — Quiet observational)

drift, settle, rest, hold, pool, gather, unfold, ease, fold, lift, pour, set, place, lower, raise, push, pull, hold, lean, trace, ripple, breathe, soften, dim, brighten, catch, pass.

Documentary verbs describe the present moment without forcing it. Subjects DO things naturally; the camera observes.

---

## Camera grammar defaults

- **Establishing:** wide locked-off or very slow stabilized push-in
- **Subject beats:** medium 35–50mm with controlled handheld for "presence"
- **Detail beats:** macro 90–100mm, locked-off, allows the audience to study the subject
- **Environmental beats:** wide stabilized lateral or aerial drone descent
- **Reaction beats:** medium close-up controlled handheld, subject doesn't react to camera

The documentary camera is patient. It waits for moments rather than directing them.

---

## Lighting defaults

- Available natural light (window, sun, practical sources)
- Warm directional raking sun (golden hour signature)
- Deep preserved shadows with detail
- Tyndall effect on dust in beams
- Halation on highlights (suggests film stock)

Avoid: studio softbox setups, three-point lighting, dramatic single-source key (these read as drama, not documentary).

---

## Color grade

- Kodak Portra warmth on skin
- Slight magenta-warm cast in shadows
- Slight teal in deep shadows
- Halation on highlights (key film signature)
- Fine grain (35mm) or heavier grain (16mm for archival feel)
- Slightly desaturated saturation overall (documentary doesn't pop colors)

---

## Atmospheric vocabulary

Documentary atmosphere lives in environment specifics:

- Worn pine plank tables, wear-grained surfaces
- Dust motes drifting in late-afternoon light
- Tyndall effect through windows
- Half-open burlap sacks, brass-handled tools
- Soft heat-haze in summer environments
- Cool blue mist in early-morning environments
- Practical lighting visible (lamps, candles, hearths)

These details earn the heritage register without saying "this is heritage."

---

## Sound design

Documentary sound is observational:

- Natural ambient (room tone, weather, distant activity)
- Subject's own movements audible (footsteps, fabric rustle, breath)
- Practical sounds in environment (clock, fire, wind, water)
- VO if present is calm and measured, never theatrical
- Music sparse, often single instrument or solo voice

```
SFX: ambient room tone, distant breeze, single floor creak, fire crackle in hearth, soft fabric rustle, occasional clock tick, rain on window glass.
```

---

## What documentary "shows" vs what it tells

The documentary register tells nothing. Everything is shown.

✅ "She unwraps the fabric carefully, fingers tracing the edge of the embroidery."
❌ "She remembers her grandmother every time she touches this fabric."

✅ "The brass scale tilts slightly as the almond settles on the pan, the dial needle finding equilibrium."
❌ "This scale has been in the family since 1967, a testament to enduring craftsmanship."

If the brief calls for context that can only be told (a history, a memory, a fact), it goes in the VO track — not in the prompt's visual description.

### As modifier on `science-education`
- "Observational science documentary"
- Real subjects (researchers, equipment, processes) in real environments

---

## What this genre does NOT do

- Theatrical performance from on-camera people
- Choreographed action with visible direction
- Dramatic lighting setups (single hard key, color gels)
- Heavy color grades that look digital
- Fast-cut rhythms (documentary breathes)
- Action-driven climaxes (documentary climaxes are observational reveals)

---

## Beat density

Documentary breathes more than any other genre.

- 7s scene: 2–3 cuts (held longer beats)
- 11s scene: 3–4 cuts
- 15s scene: 4–5 cuts

The documentary held shot is the genre's signature. Where commercial would cut, documentary holds.

## Common scenes

- Worker at a process — hands at work, environment around them
- Founder portrait — face in window light, slight smile, natural expression
- Heritage object detail — brass scale, leather notebook, brass scoop
- Environmental establishing — facility exterior at golden hour, weathered building
- Archival reconstruction — period objects on a wooden table, dust in light beam, single subject in frame
- Family / community moment — group around a table, conversation, soft warm light
- Process beat — material being transformed (dough rising, fabric folded, wood planed)

---

## Heritage palette specifically

When the brief calls for heritage / archival / "since 1967" / "family-built" register:

- Worn pine plank tables (lots of wear-grain visible)
- Half-open burlap sacks with non-specific cotton stencil markings
- Brass mechanical tools (scoop, scale, ladle) with honey patina
- Vintage cast-iron equipment (matte black with knuckle wear)
- Coil of cotton twine, folded canvas apron in muted earth tones
- Late-afternoon warm directional sun with strong Tyndall effect on drifting dust
- Deep preserved shadows, fine film grain
- Negative space held clean for archival photo composite in post (upper-right third, lower horizontal band)

This is the Hayden Valley Scene 8 visual vocabulary. Use it whenever the brief calls for heritage register.

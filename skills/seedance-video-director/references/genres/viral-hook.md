# Viral hook

> **Lens note.** The camera stacks in this file are grade, stock and finish templates. Their millimeter values are legacy — convert the lens to a FOV degree from the ladder in `optics.md` before writing (`47° (50mm)`, `29° (85mm)`, `84° (24mm)`). Millimeters never lead, and apertures, ISO and lens brand names control nothing.


Attention is the hero. Hook in the first 1.5 seconds, sustain through fast pacing. Use as base genre for: TikTok / Reels / Shorts content, social media native ads, autoplay-feed video, mobile-first vertical, hook-first content where a 1-second view counts as engagement.

---

## Structural priorities (when used as base)

1. **First 1.5 seconds is the entire battle.** If the hook doesn't land, the rest doesn't matter. Front-load the visual / kinetic / curiosity peak.
2. **Sustain not surge.** Energy stays high throughout — no slow build, no held silences, no quiet beats.
3. **Vertical-friendly composition.** Default to 9:16. Action happens within the vertical column.
4. **Loop-ready.** End frame matches start frame so autoplay loops feel intentional.

## Verb register (Tier 3 — Kinetic / energetic)

snap, flick, jerk, whip, bounce, lurch, hop, leap, dart, sprint, dash, crash, slap, pound, drum, beat, surge, burst, pulse, throb, race, rush, charge, swerve, weave, dodge, duck, vault, plant, slam, hit, strike, punch, kick.

Viral verbs are present, kinetic, and immediate. Action verbs in present tense, no past or progressive.

---

## Camera grammar defaults

- **Hook beat (0–1.5s):** the most attention-grabbing camera move available — fast push-in, snap zoom, whip pan to subject, sudden reveal
- **Sustain beats:** controlled handheld with constant motion, fast lateral tracks, dutch angles for energy
- **Pacing:** snap cuts every 0.5–1 second
- **End beat:** match the opening composition for loop-ready autoplay

The viral camera never holds for more than 1 second. Holding kills the autoplay swipe.

---

## Hook patterns

The first 1.5 seconds. Pick one (or compose):

### "Mid-action open"
Open already in the action's peak. No setup, no establishment.

> Open: She's MID-AIR, already falling. Camera tracks her descent. We don't see what she jumped from.

### "Surprise visual"
Open with something unexpected — color, scale, movement that doesn't make sense.

> Open: A massive watermelon on a tiny dining table. Just sitting there. The fork next to it looks comically small.

### "Question hook"
Open with a visual that implies a question the viewer wants answered.

> Open: A phone screen filling the frame, a single text message: "Don't open the door."

### "Direct address"
Open with the subject looking directly into the camera, addressing the viewer.

> Open: Front-facing camera, she stares directly at the lens. Speaks: "You won't believe this."

### "Action transition"
Open with a transition mid-beat — door slamming open, water splashing, something exploding.

> Open: Door SLAMS OPEN. Camera RUSHES through. Inside: a perfectly lit empty room.

### "Pattern interrupt"
Open with a familiar visual that breaks expectation.

> Open: A normal coffee shop. Camera pans to the counter. The barista is a robot.

## Lighting defaults

- Bright, high-key for daylight scenes (overexposed highlights are okay)
- Saturated practical lighting for nighttime / interior
- Strong color contrast (warm key against cool environment)
- Anamorphic flares on every visible light source (for cinematic-tier viral)
- Mobile-camera quality lighting for UGC-tier viral

---

## Color grade

- Oversaturated commercial grade
- Bright skin tones
- Hyper-blue skies, hyper-green plants
- Slight teal-and-orange (modern social-grade)
- High contrast (no flat midtones)

For UGC-tier viral, the grade is more muted (mobile-camera flat) — but for production-tier viral, push saturation hard.

---

## Cut frequency

Viral cuts hard and fast.

- 7s scene: 6–8 cuts
- 15s scene: 12–15 cuts (Seedance hard cap is around 8 cuts per generation, so for 12+ cut viral you may need to split into two generations)

The cut is the energy carrier. Don't hold any single composition for more than 1 second.

---

## Beat density

Each beat (0.5–1 second) needs to land:
- A visual (something to look at)
- A change (something different from the previous beat)
- A reason to keep watching (curiosity, payoff anticipation, surprise)

Three beats per second of viral content. Higher than any other genre.

---

## Loop-ready endings

The end frame should match the start frame composition so autoplay loops smoothly.

Pattern: open with [composition X], drive through the scene, end with [composition X again, slightly altered].

Example: open with the watermelon on the table → various beats happen → end with the watermelon on the table looking exactly the same except a small bite is missing.

---

## Sound design

Viral sound is loud, present, and rhythmic.

- Music with strong beat (cuts hit on the beat)
- SFX punchy and exaggerated (whoosh on every cut, snap on every reveal)
- Voice clear and high-energy when talking-head present
- Ambient minimal (music carries it)

```
SFX: hook whoosh, beat-aligned snap on each cut, oversized impact sound on the climax, single sustained note at the close, loop-ready return tone.
```

## Composition with other genres

### As modifier on `commercial-product`
- "Viral commercial" — product showcase optimized for autoplay feeds
- Front-loaded product reveal in the hook
- Loops to product at the close

### As modifier on `ugc-authentic`
- TikTok-native viral content
- Hook in the first 1.5s, casual sustain through the rest

### As modifier on `action-intense`
- Fast-paced action optimized for social
- Loops on the action beat
- Higher cut density than standalone action

### As modifier on `explainer-clarity`
- "Did you know" / "here's how" educational viral
- Hook with a question or surprise fact
- Sustains by delivering info quickly

---

## What this genre does NOT do

- Slow build (kills the swipe)
- Held silence (autoplay swipe killer)
- Long single takes (viral wants cuts)
- Cinematic restraint (saves nothing)
- Subtle subtext (viral is loud)

---

## Negative prompt block

```
Negative: no slow build, no held silences, no fade-in/fade-out, no commercial-quality slow pace, no cinematic restraint, no watermarks, no subtitles unless explicitly part of the on-screen text design.
```

## Common scenes

- Mid-action hook with no setup
- Question / mystery hook
- Direct-address talking head with high-energy delivery
- Pattern interrupt visual
- Transformation reveal in the first 2 seconds
- Comedy beat (visual gag, unexpected outcome)
- Product reveal with maximum visual impact
- Trend-aligned hook (matches a current TikTok format)

---

## A few platform-specific notes

- **TikTok:** 9:16, audio-driven, hooks favor music-aligned cuts
- **Reels:** 9:16, visual-driven, slightly more polished than TikTok
- **Shorts:** 9:16, YouTube algorithm favors retention so beats can be slightly slower than TikTok
- **Threads / X video:** 16:9 or 1:1 acceptable, hooks favor text-on-screen + visual combo
- **Square Instagram feed:** 1:1, slower-paced viral okay (the viewer is paused on it)

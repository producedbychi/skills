# Prompt principles

Apply the guidance relevant to the shot. Keep the user's requested action, perspective, and visual style.

## Visible actions

Replace abstract emotion with a visible change. Instead of "she looks stressed," describe shoulders lifting, a locked jaw, an exhale through the nose, and eyes fixed on the door. Instead of "a dangerous alley," describe its light source, wet brick, standing water, and visible occupants.

Use measurements where scale, speed, visibility, or distance affects the action. Choose values the action can plausibly sustain. Emotion can appear through muscle movement; a face can remain still when the shot calls for it.

Read [action vocabulary](antislop.md) when choosing verbs for a particular intensity. State how the action starts, changes, and settles.

## Desired state and constraints

State the desired behavior first. "Locked-off tripod with steady frame edges" communicates a stable camera. "The only figures are @sol_ref and @daye_ref" defines occupancy.

Pair a necessary exclusion with the state it protects. For repeated documented failures, a short negative constraint can be useful. Keep text suppression, reflection controls, and music exclusions with their corresponding blocks and only when they fit the request.

For locked-perspective formats, specify the continuity explicitly. POV can use "no cuts, no zoom, natural head movement." A continuous shot can use "single continuous shot, no transitions, no time skips."

## Camera and openings

Match camera behavior to the action. A quiet beat may use a locked frame; a reveal may use a controlled pull; an intense beat may need a lateral track or handheld motion. Vary the camera only when the beat warrants it.

Start with the action already in progress when the brief leaves the opening open. Preserve a requested setup, reveal, starting frame, or ending frame.

## Identity and references

Describe subjects by role, build, wardrobe, and visible identity markers rather than numerical age. An attached identity image carries likeness; the prompt adds state, orientation, placement, and contact points. Use the user-confirmed tags.

Write the video prompt body in English. Keep upload instructions and production specifications outside that body.

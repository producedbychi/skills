# Creative brief mode

The brief is the shared definition of the assignment and the test for later creative work. It says what must change, for whom, and within what boundaries. It is not a treatment, script, shot list, or contract. Use the client's brief if one exists; repair material gaps or conflicts instead of rewriting sound decisions.

## Discover the assignment

Read the supplied request, brand and audience material, campaign or product facts, distribution plans, available assets, prior decisions, and feedback. Trace consequential claims and constraints to a source. Ask about gaps that would change the objective, primary audience, scope, budget, deadline, or approver. Avoid a long intake questionnaire when a few decisions will settle the direction.

Use one of two honest statuses:

- **Working brief:** internal alignment draft. It can include proposed answers, clearly named assumptions, and decisions needed before the client approves a direction.
- **Client-review brief:** concise enough to read and approve, but specific enough to guide concept selection, scope planning, and feedback. Its strategic essentials are confirmed or explicitly put to the client as choices. A polished document with unresolved essentials is still a working brief.

## Write the brief

Lead with the assignment in plain language: what is being made, why now, and what decision this brief asks the client to make. Then cover the following in a readable order. Combine fields for a small project; separate deliverables in a table when there are several.

| Decision area | Brief must make clear |
| --- | --- |
| Problem or opportunity | The current situation and the change the work is meant to create; distinguish evidence from an inferred diagnosis. |
| Objective and evidence of success | The primary outcome, desired audience response, and a measure or observable signal when one is supplied or can be proposed without pretending it is agreed. |
| Audience and insight | Primary audience, relevant context, current belief or obstacle, and why this message would matter to them. Segment audiences only when their needs differ materially. |
| Single main message and proof | The one point the audience should retain, with sourced facts, product demonstrations, or other support. Separate mandatory claims from possible creative wording. |
| Deliverables and distribution | Each asset's role, channel, audience, call to action, duration or format when decided, and how multiple assets connect. Video, email, and landing page are separate deliverables, not one vague campaign line. |
| Boundaries | Brand, legal or claim review, accessibility, required assets, technical specifications, exclusions, and production constraints that are genuinely supplied or necessary to decide. |
| Resources and process | Budget or range, due dates, client inputs, decision-maker, feedback route, and approval milestones when known. Mark missing commercial terms as unresolved rather than filling them in. |

End with a short **decision request**: what the client can approve now, what remains open, who must answer it, and whether those answers affect concept, scope, schedule, or price. Put detailed source notes and operational risks outside the client-facing narrative when possible.

## Test it before handoff

- Can an unfamiliar client explain the assignment, target audience, intended effect, deliverables, and their own next decision after reading it once?
- Is there one prioritized communication task rather than a collection of unrelated goals or messages?
- Does every proof point have a source or a visible verification task? Are proposed targets clearly labeled?
- Could two creative teams propose different solutions that both satisfy the brief? If not, the brief may be prescribing the treatment.
- Are scope and constraints specific enough to inform an estimate, while leaving fees and contractual obligations to commercial documents?

If any essential answer is missing, deliver a working brief and ask the smallest set of decision-changing questions. Do not label it approved or ready to send merely because every heading is filled.

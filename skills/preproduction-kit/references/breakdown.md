# Breakdown mode

The breakdown turns a treatment, script, or approved concept into a feasible capture and asset plan. It is a bridge to scheduling and estimating, not a list of attractive shots. In `full`, use the [AV script](script.md) as the beat map. For a standalone breakdown without a script, work from the supplied direction and mark creative decisions `provisional`. Do not invent a treatment just to satisfy an order of operations.

## Build the plan

Start with a deliverable/version inventory and the beats or claims each version must carry. For each beat, identify the picture, audio, graphics, and proof needed in the edit. Assign a stable beat ID from the script and a distinct shot or asset ID where useful. Separate existing material, proposed capture, licensed material, graphics, and generated material. A reference image is not an available production asset unless its source and intended use are known. Choose detail based on the project: a short product reel may need a lean shot list; a multi-location campaign may need a setup plan, asset register, and handoff notes.

Use a working table with these fields where they matter:

| Beat and item ID | Edit purpose and specific action or asset | Source and owner | Setup, sound, and format needs | Dependency, clearance, or status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B01 / S01 | What the viewer must see or hear, what happens, and coverage or framing needed | Capture, client asset, licensed asset, graphic, or proposed AI media, with a named owner if known | Location/setup, people/props, sync sound, and target formats when known | Approval, access, rights, test, or unresolved item |

Separate material that exists from material still needed. A client-supplied file may be usable for concepting while its usage rights remain `unknown`. For AI media, record the intended shot, identity and product references, continuity requirements, and rights questions. Do not write provider-specific prompts unless the user asks for them.

Group capture by setup after mapping the edit. For each setup, note location, people, products/props, wardrobe or continuity, equipment or special access, and the shots it serves when these details affect feasibility. Include coverage that lets the editor connect beats: wide and close views where useful, action starts and finishes, reactions, inserts, clean product views, room tone or sound effects, and graphics-safe space. Avoid padding the list with generic shots. Check whether each factual claim has visual or audio support.

Check whether the planned material can support the requested runtime and versions. Note reframe risks for vertical or square delivery, text-safe areas, language versions, and alternate calls to action. A single locked-off horizontal shot is not automatically sufficient for every crop. For non-video deliverables, list copy, design, proof, form or link, and approval dependencies without pretending they are shots.

## Handoff output

Deliver the shot or asset table plus the supporting information the team actually needs:

- Capture groupings, location or setup order, estimated effort, and crew or equipment needs when applicable; label unbooked resources as proposed.
- Client inputs, graphics, music, voice, stock, and other assets, with owners and delivery dates if confirmed.
- Review and approval points, tests, permissions, and dependencies that affect the schedule.
- Risks and alternatives for material that may not be available.
- A clear distinction between confirmed bookings or dates and proposed plans.

For a standalone breakdown, include a script or storyboard only when requested or needed to resolve an actual production ambiguity. Do not add a full call sheet, production schedule, or technical specification by default.

## Quality check

- Every required beat and deliverable maps to obtainable material.
- The plan tells a producer what to source or capture and tells an editor why each item matters.
- Every shot or asset is specific enough to estimate or assign; no generic B-roll conceals a missing story beat.
- Requested formats and runtime have plausible coverage.
- Resource, schedule, and rights dependencies have owners or explicit unknowns.
- A scope change is flagged for paperwork rather than silently added to the job.

# Rights and releases

Use a rights schedule to make permitted uses and missing permissions visible. A supplied file or verbal assurance is not proof of clearance.

For each material or person, record the source or rights holder, exact asset or performance, who obtained permission, where the record is stored, and the permitted media, territory, term, platforms, edits, paid advertising, and exclusivity if applicable. Record restrictions and expiration dates. Use `confirmed`, `needs evidence`, `not requested`, or `out of scope` as status labels; never change an unknown right to confirmed by inference.

Check talent and voice, music composition and recording, stock media, artwork, logos, locations, client-supplied assets, and AI-generated material when relevant. For AI uses, surface consent for a person's likeness or voice, reuse of supplied material, and any training or model-input restrictions. An agreement draft may describe these issues, but a lawyer or rights holder must settle terms and permissions.

The release checklist is a task list, not a claim that releases have been signed. Keep a separate line for each person, location, or asset that requires evidence.

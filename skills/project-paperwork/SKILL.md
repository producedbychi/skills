# Project paperwork

Prepare the requested documents from one set of project facts. Drafts require human review, and this skill does not send, sign, file, or collect payment.

## Establish the terms

Read the brief, approved scope, client correspondence, supplied forms, and any company-approved templates. Record the parties, project, jurisdiction, deliverables and versions, schedule, revisions, fees, expenses, payment milestones, ownership, licensed uses, and approvers. Mark each term as confirmed, proposed, or unresolved. Ask for missing information when guessing would change a price, obligation, permission, or due date.

Choose the documents the user needs. For a full project package, consider an estimate, scope of work or agreement draft, rights schedule, release checklist, and payment schedule; create a change order or invoice when the project stage calls for one. Read only the relevant guidance:

- [Agreements and scope](references/agreements-and-scope.md) for estimates, scopes, agreements, and change orders.
- [Rights and releases](references/rights-and-releases.md) for licensed uses, talent, music, locations, supplied assets, and AI material.
- [Billing](references/billing.md) for invoices and payment schedules.

Prefer a user-supplied or counsel-approved agreement template. If none exists, produce a clearly labeled working draft or term sheet with unresolved terms visible; do not present it as ready to sign. Do not invent tax rules, statutory language, or permissions. Check current authoritative sources when a jurisdiction-specific rule is material, and flag it for a qualified professional.

## Cross-check before delivery

Check that party names, project name, scope, deliverable counts and formats, dates, revisions, fees, payment terms, and usage fields agree across documents. Recalculate every amount with a reliable calculator or script. Flag missing or conflicting terms instead of silently choosing one version. Record which template and source facts informed each draft.

For likeness, voice, music, stock, and AI-generated material, do not claim rights are cleared without supporting documentation. Identify who must confirm or obtain permission. Contracts and taxes need review by the client's lawyer or accountant where appropriate; the skill can prepare drafts and issue lists, not certify compliance.
